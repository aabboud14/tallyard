## 1. EU construction and demolition waste (CDW): volumes, recovery, reuse, the 70% target and its revision, and the Circular Economy Act

### Takeaway
In 2022 the EU generated 2,233 Mt of waste, and construction accounted for 38.4% (about 857 Mt including excavated soil). Mineral CDW excluding soil runs at roughly 340 to 375 Mt a year (2014 to 2018 data). The 70% recovery target was met long ago (about 89% of mineral CDW recovered), but most of that is low-grade backfilling and crushed aggregate. Reuse of building elements is about 1% or less. No revision of the CDW target has been adopted. The Circular Economy Act proposal is due by the end of 2026, and construction groups are lobbying for reuse to be central to it; I could not confirm that the proposal has been published.

### Cited Findings
**Generation (Eurostat env_wasgen, reference year 2022)**
- Total waste generated in the EU by all economic activities and households in 2022: 2,233 million tonnes, or 4,991 kg per capita (load-bearing). Source: [Eurostat, Waste statistics, Statistics Explained](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Waste_statistics)
- Construction contributed 38.4% of total EU waste in 2022, ahead of mining and quarrying at 22.7% (load-bearing). Source: [Eurostat, Waste statistics](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Waste_statistics). Statista also publishes a construction share series ([Statista, construction waste as share of all waste EU](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1399099/construction-waste-as-a-share-of-all-waste-generated-in-the-eu/)), paywalled.
- 795 Mt of waste excluding major mineral wastes was generated in the EU in 2022, 36% of the total. Most CDW mineral waste is classed as "major mineral waste", so shares change a lot depending on whether that category is included. Source: [Eurostat, Waste statistics](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Waste_statistics)
- In 2022, 1,992 Mt of waste was treated in the EU (all waste streams), 61.4% recovered and 38.6% disposed of. Source: [Eurostat news, Growth in waste recovery in 2022, 17 October 2024](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20241017-1)

**CDW excluding excavated soil (older reference years; no 2022 non-soil figure was found)**
- 374 Mt of CDW excluding excavated soil in the EU in 2016 (EEA briefing). Source: [Energy in Demand summary of the EEA briefing on CDW, January 2020](https://energyindemand.com/2020/01/17/new-eea-briefing-on-construction-and-demolition-waste/)
- 371,910 thousand tonnes of mineral waste from construction and demolition in the EU in 2018. This is a secondary source citing Eurostat. Source: [Wikipedia, Concrete recycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete_recycling)
- 336 Mt of CDW excluding excavations in the EU in 2014. Source: [Villoria Saez and Osmani, A diagnosis of CDW generation and recovery practice in the EU, Journal of Cleaner Production 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652619332706). The search extract did not show which of the returned academic papers this figure came from, so check it.
- A Nordic Council report citing the European Commission gives 450 to 500 Mt of CDW a year in the EU. Source: [Nordic Council of Ministers, TemaNord 2023:544, Looking outside the Nordics](https://pub.norden.org/temanord2023-544/4-looking-outside-the-nordics.html)

**Recovery, recycling, backfilling and reuse**
- The Eurostat CDW recovery indicator counts CDW prepared for reuse, recycled or otherwise materially recovered, including backfilling, divided by CDW treated. It covers non-hazardous mineral CDW. The dataset cei_wm040 was discontinued on 16 August 2023. Sources: [data.europa.eu, Recovery rate of CDW](https://data.europa.eu/data/datasets/uczdo4z1o5qcllbdtbkhq?locale=en); [DBnomics, Eurostat cei_wm040](https://db.nomics.world/Eurostat/cei_wm040)
- The EU average recovery rate is about 89%, but "current recovery uses mainly low-quality recycled aggregates for backfilling material or road construction sub-bases in the best-case scenario". Source: [European Commission JRC, Techno-economic and environmental assessment of CDW (JRC135470)](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/sites/default/files/2024-01/JRC135470_01_1.pdf)
- A JRC-based policy text says the 70% target has been reached EU-wide, with 89% of the mineral fraction recovered in 2022 (load-bearing). Source: [JRC, Policy measures to promote reuse and high-quality recycling (copy hosted January 2026)](https://www.shareyourgreendesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/TEXT.pdf)
- Another paper cites an official Eurostat figure of 77% and says recycling is overestimated because backfilling is vaguely defined. The WFD does not define backfilling, and member states count it inconsistently as recovery or as disposal. Sources: [ResearchGate, Critical review of the recovery rates of CDW in the EU, 2023](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371275399_Critical_review_of_the_recovery_rates_of_construction_and_demolition_waste_in_the_European_Union_-_An_analysis_of_influencing_factors_in_selected_EU_countries); [ScienceDirect, same paper, Waste Management 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0956053X23003616)
- A 2026 study finds that most member states exceed the 70% CDW recovery target but "much of this recovery is achieved through low-value recycling methods such as backfilling". Source: [ScienceDirect, What drives high-value CDW recycling in the EU?, 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949750726000246)
- Reuse: only 1% of building elements are reused after their first application in North-West Europe. Source: [Interreg NWE FCRBE, statistical analysis v15](https://vb.nweurope.eu/media/16598/statistical-analysis-v15.pdf)
- National example: Italy's CDW recovery and recycling rate was 79.8% in 2022. Source: [ISPRA environmental indicators](https://indicatoriambientali.isprambiente.it/en/second-half/192)

**Waste Framework Directive (WFD) target and revision**
- The WFD (2008) set a target of 70% by weight for preparing for reuse, recycling and other material recovery (including backfilling) of non-hazardous CDW by 2020. Sources: [European Demolition Association, Waste Framework Directive](https://www.europeandemolition.org/information/construction-and-circular-economy/waste-framework-directive); [ECOS, EU waste frameworks review paper, March 2025](https://ecostandard.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2025-03-31_WFD-WEEE-Dir-review_ECOS.pdf)
- The 2018 amendment required the Commission to consider new preparation for reuse and recycling targets for CDW and its material-specific fractions by 31 December 2024. Sources: [EDA, EU targets for demolition waste](https://www.europeandemolition.org/communication/news/eu-targets-demolition-waste); [Interreg Europe, Policy brief on sustainable construction, March 2024](https://www.interregeurope.eu/sites/default/files/2024-03/Policy%20brief%20on%20Sustainable%20construction.pdf)
- No adopted revision of the CDW target was found. ECOS proposes separate reuse targets, material-specific targets (steel, cement and concrete) and mandatory pre-demolition audits. Sources: [ECOS WFD review paper 2025](https://ecostandard.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2025-03-31_WFD-WEEE-Dir-review_ECOS.pdf); [ECOS response on the Circular Economy Act, October 2025](https://ecostandard.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025-10_ECOS-response_CE-Act-web-final_SUBMITTED.pdf)
- A secondary site says member states must reach 70% "by the end of 2025". This conflicts with the 2020 date in the directive and is probably wrong. Source: [Sustainability Directory news](https://news.sustainability-directory.com/circularity/eu-policy-mandates-70-construction-waste-recovery-to-secure-material-supply/)

**Circular Economy Act (CEA)**
- IEEP: the CEA proposal is "due by the end of 2026" and is meant to enable the free movement of secondary raw materials and stimulate demand for them. It is linked to the revision of the Construction Products Regulation. Source: [IEEP, The Circular Economy Act, material consumption targets and secondary material use, June 2025](https://ieep.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/The-Circular-Economy-Act-material-consumption-targets-and-secondary-material-use-IEEP-2025.pdf)
- The public consultation was launched in August 2025. An earlier 2025 timetable aimed for the first half of 2026. Sources: [EUWID, Commission launches consultation on CEA, 6 August 2025](https://www.euwid-recycling.com/news/policy/european-commission-launches-public-consultation-on-upcoming-circular-economy-act-060825/); [ERP Recycling, CEA public consultation expected, June 2025](https://erp-recycling.org/ie/news-and-events/2025/06/circular-economy-act-public-consultation-expected/)
- Stated aims: a single market for secondary raw materials and certain waste flows, and harmonised end-of-waste criteria. Today there are 27 national end-of-waste regimes. Construction Products Europe warns that a one-size-fits-all approach will not work for construction. Sources: [Construction Products Europe, Circular Economy Act](https://construction-products.eu/publications/circular-economy-act/); [ESG Dive, EU seeking input on CEA](https://www.esgdive.com/news/european-union-seeking-input-circular-economy-act-clean-industrial-deal-epr-circularity/757320/)
- A coalition of 91 organisations asks for circular construction to be "a cornerstone" of the CEA. Its priorities are reuse in preference to demolition and new build, public procurement to create demand, and harmonised end-of-waste criteria. Source: [European Circular Economy Stakeholder Platform news](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/en/news-and-events/all-news/91-organisations-urge-eu-make-circular-construction-cornerstone-circular-economy-act)
- The Nordic Capital Regions (Region Hovedstaden) ask that products demolished intact for reuse or remanufacture should not be classed as waste. Sources: [Region H press release](https://www.regionh.dk/english/press-and-news/latest-news/Pages/Regions-are-putting-pressure-on-the-EU-The-construction-industry-must-play-a-greater-role-in-the-new-Circular-Economy-Act.aspx); [Region H enclosure](https://edagsorden.regionh.dk/cms/HtmlPublication-9456/enclosures/25.pdf)
- ERRIN (November 2025) recommends a mandatory pre-demolition audit. INERTWASTE asks for methods that match forecast demand for secondary materials with CDW supply. Sources: [ERRIN input to the CEA](https://errin.eu/system/files/2025-11/ERRIN%20Input%20-%20Circular%20Economy%20Act.pdf); [Interreg Europe INERTWASTE](https://www.interregeurope.eu/inertwaste/news-and-events/news/inertwaste-shares-input-for-upcoming-eu-circular-economy-act)
- A low-quality secondary source reports a CEA goal of doubling the circular material use rate from 12% to 24% by 2030. This echoes the 2020 Circular Economy Action Plan ambition. Source: [SCI.AI news](https://news.yrules.com/en/archives/13670)

### Inferences
- Construction-sector waste in 2022 was about 857 Mt (38.4% of 2,233 Mt; my arithmetic). Mineral CDW excluding soil was about 370 Mt in 2016 to 2018. So excavated soil and other construction waste make up roughly 480 Mt, more than half the construction total. For Tallyard the relevant base is the non-soil fraction, and within that only demolition and strip-out of buildings (not civil engineering), which is a minority of the 370 Mt. No source split it this way.
- "Recovery" statistics say almost nothing about reuse. The useful anchors are the roughly 1% NWE element reuse rate (FCRBE) and the French figures in section 5 (0.3% to 0.5% of building waste). Treat the current EU reuse rate for building products as under 1% by mass.
- The policy gap is the opportunity: the 70% target is met through backfilling, so pressure is shifting to reuse-specific targets and pre-demolition audits (ECOS, ERRIN, the 91-organisation coalition). If the CEA or a WFD revision adopts them, they would export the French model (section 3) EU-wide. On the evidence found, that would not take effect before 2028 to 2030.

### Gaps
- No 2022 Eurostat figure was retrieved for mineral CDW alone (waste category W121) or for the CDW recovery rate under the successor to cei_wm040. Pull env_wasgen (NACE F, W121 versus W126 soils) and env_wastrt (backfilling versus recycling for mineral CDW) directly.
- Eurostat does not report a "reuse" share of CDW separately in these tables; reused elements usually never become waste.
- CEA status as at October 2026: I could not confirm whether the Commission has published the proposal, or whether it has construction-specific provisions. One article is titled ["Europe's Circular Economy Act has entered the implementation phase"](https://www.openaccessgovernment.org/europes-circular-economy-act-has-entered-the-implementation-phase/209680/), but its content was not seen. One search summary said the CEA rests on three pillars (EPR reform, a Landfill Directive amendment and a WEEE recast) with no CDW pillar; I could not tie that claim to a specific page.

## 2. EU construction output, construction materials value, the non-residential demolition and renovation segments, and office obsolescence

### Takeaway
FIEC puts EU27 construction investment at about EUR 1,821 bn in 2024 (about GBP 1,566 bn). A different FIEC page shows EUR 1,422 bn, so check which is right. Output fell 2.0% to 2.5% in 2024 on a housing slump (minus 7.7%). Non-residential was flat (plus 0.1%) and civil engineering grew 5.9%. Germany (EUR 464 bn) and France (EUR 258 bn) are the largest markets. ## 3. Regulation that forces or rewards reuse, by country and at EU level

### Takeaway
France is the clear leader in regulation that forces reuse. It requires a mandatory pre-demolition resource audit (PEMD, over 1,000 m2, since 1 July 2023), funds reuse through an extended producer responsibility scheme with reuse targets (REP PMCB, 2% to 5% targets but about 0.2% actual), and sets public procurement duties. Denmark (tightening whole-life carbon caps 2025, 2027, 2029) and Finland (mandatory demolition material report since 1 January 2025) are next. Sweden and the Netherlands are slower or stalled. Germany's product law still blocks reuse. EU-wide, the EPBD whole-life carbon disclosure (2028 and 2030) and the CPR recast's coverage of used products are the levers that will generalise demand.

### Cited Findings
**France**
- PEMD diagnostic (products, equipment, materials and waste) is mandatory for demolitions and significant renovations of buildings with cumulative floor area over 1,000 m2 where the permit or works authorisation was filed (or contract accepted) after 1 July 2023 (load-bearing). Source: [FFB, PEMD diagnostic mandatory from 1 July 2023](https://www.ffbatiment.fr/actualites-batiment/actualite/demolition-et-renovation-significative-le-diagnostic-pemd-obligatoire-au-1er-juillet-2023)
- Content: nature, quantity and location of materials, products and equipment, their condition, and the options for reuse on site, on another site or through reuse channels. A post-works form (CERFA 16288*01) must record the quantities actually reused and recovered. The diagnostic (CERFA 16287*01) and that form are filed with CSTB on the PEMD platform. Legal basis: decree 2021-821 of 25 June 2021 and the order of 26 March 2023. Sources: [CD2E reuse working group, June 2024](https://cd2e.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/27062024-1er-GT-CTB-reemploi.pdf); [Valobat guidance on the PEMD diagnostic](https://valobat.fr/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Valobat-MOA-MOE-diagnostic-PEMD.pdf); [TUV SUD, what is the PEMD diagnostic](https://www.tuvsud.com/fr-fr/presse-et-medias/2023/november/quest-ce-que-le-diagnostic-pemd); [arrete of 26 March 2023 (AIDA INERIS)](https://aida.ineris.fr/sites/aida/files/2023-05/a20230326_1_3_0.pdf)
- REP PMCB (extended producer responsibility for building products and materials): created by the AGEC law. The eco-organisations' specification was set by the interministerial order of 10 June 2022. The four eco-organisations are Ecomaison, Ecominero, Valdelia and Valobat, with OCA Batiment as coordinator. Sources: [Ecologie.gouv.fr, consolidated REP PMCB specification](https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/Cahier%20des%20charges%20consolid%C3%A9%20-%20REP%20PMCB.pdf); [Valobat, specification published in the Journal officiel](https://www.valobat.fr/actus-valobat/le-cahier-des-charges-de-la-rep-pmcb-publie-au-journal-officiel/); [OCAB presentation, Normandy](https://neci.normandie.fr/sites/default/files/2024-04/OCAB_Contrat_Collectivites_PMCB_webinaire_diff.pdf)
- REP PMCB reuse targets: Ecominero aims for a 5% reuse and reutilisation rate by 2028, 90% material recovery of inert waste by 2028 (76% currently) and 45% recycling. Valobat cites 2% in 2024 and 4% in 2027. A 2026 note on the scheme's reform gives 3% and 5% for 2024 to 2027 and 2027 to 2030 (extract truncated). Sources: [Ecominero press release, Assises des dechets, September 2023](https://www.ecominero.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/cp-ecominero-assises-des-dechets-v1909.pdf); [Ecominero 4R presentation, November 2023](https://cd2e.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Support_Ecominero_4R_14112023.pdf); [Valobat webinar, Novabuild, 2024](https://novabuild.fr/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/VALOBAT-Marine-Webinaire-Novabuild.pdf); [Intercommunalites de France, note on the REP PMCB reform, April 2026](https://www.intercommunalites.fr/app/uploads/1/2026/04/Note-detaillee-Cahier-des-Charges-REP-PMCB.pdf)
- Actual performance: ADEME BatiZoom says the reuse rate is at or under 0.2% for both waste categories, below the 2% target (load-bearing). Source: [ADEME BatiZoom, REP PMCB indicator](https://batizoom.ademe.fr/indicateurs/rep-pmcb-taux-de-recyclage-par-type-de-materiaux)
- The scheme is being reformed for 2027 ("refondation"). A Senate question in 2026 raises "dysfunctions". A bill sought to take wood products out of REP PMCB. Sources: [Decideurs Immo, REP PMCB from founding scheme to the 2027 reform](https://www.decideurs-immo.com/paroles-d-experts/64937-la-filiere-rep-pmcb-du-dispositif-fondateur-a-la-refondation-de-2027.html); [Senate written question 2026](https://www.senat.fr/questions/base/2026/qSEQ260408415.html); [Senate report 591 (2024-2025)](https://www.senat.fr/rap/l24-591/l24-5914.html)
- Public procurement (AGEC article 58): decree 2021-254 required public buyers to buy shares of 37 products in 17 families from reuse, reutilisation or recycled sources, mostly 20% to 40%, supplies only. It was replaced by decree 2024-134 of 21 February 2024, in force 1 July 2024, which also counts goods bought under mixed contracts and donations but still does not oblige works contracts. For building works, Code de l'environnement article L. 228-4 says buyers "must ensure the use of reused materials". Sources: [CGDD guide to AGEC article 58](https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/publications/cgdd_guide_article_58_loi_agec_0.pdf); [DREAL Nouvelle-Aquitaine reuse note](https://www.nouvelle-aquitaine.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/4_etude_dreal_eci_note_reemploi_v3.pdf); [marche-public.fr, CGDD 2024 guide](https://www.marche-public.fr/contrats-publics/Guide-cgdd-2024-article58-agec.htm); [Landot Avocats blog, June 2023](https://blog.landot-avocats.net/2023/06/22/achats-reutilises-recycles-plus-que-8-jours-pour-declarer-vos-depenses-2022/)
- The Booster du Reemploi was launched in 2020 by A4MT with about 30 partners, aiming for 25% of projects using reuse by 2024. Sources: [Batiweb, Booster aims to convert 25% of sites to reuse by 2024](https://www.batiweb.com/actualites/developpement-durable/le-booster-du-reemploi-ambitionne-de-convertir-25-des-chantiers-au-reemploi-d-ici-2024-38387); [Batiweb, Booster rolls out locally](https://www.batiweb.com/actualites/developpement-durable/le-booster-du-reemploi-se-deploie-localement-41042)

### Inferences
- Ranking of near-term markets by regulatory pull (judgement from the findings above):
  1. **France**: the only market that combines a mandatory pre-demolition resource audit with reuse reporting (PEMD), EPR money and targets for reuse (REP PMCB), and public procurement duties. Paris is close to London, and large office stock is being refurbished.
  2. **Belgium (Brussels)**: the most mature reuse dealer ecosystem (Rotor, Opalis) and public tenders with reclamation targets, but a small market.
  3. **Denmark**: the strongest carbon-limit pull; tightening in 2025, 2027 and 2029 makes reused low-carbon components valuable to designers.
  4. **Netherlands**: high stated ambition (2030 and 2050), but MPG tightening is stalled.
  5. **Finland and Norway**: new reporting and procurement rules (Finland from 2025; Norway's draft procurement standard of 2025) create data and demand, but the markets are small.
  6. **Germany**: the largest construction market (EUR 464 bn), but reuse is held back by the need for case-by-case product approvals. The NKWS and a resource passport could change that later.
  7. **Sweden**: limits not yet in law (proposed from 2027).
  8. **Switzerland**: high value, but rules are cantonal and still at the discussion stage.
- The EPBD 2028 GWP disclosure for large new buildings and the national limit roadmaps due by 1 January 2027 will spread Danish-style carbon pull EU-wide from 2028 to 2030. The CPR's inclusion of used products could either ease or complicate reuse, depending on the delegated acts. Watch both.
- The French PEMD inventory is structurally the same as Tallyard's "donor building" survey data. Tallyard could ingest PEMD outputs (CERFA 16287) as listings, and the post-works form gives measured reuse volumes.

- Iceland and Liechtenstein were not researched.

### Inferences
- **Reclaimed lumber figures are not usable.** About USD 62 bn worldwide, with Europe at USD 18 to 25 bn, far exceeds anything consistent with 1% element reuse and the French sector's 1,600 FTE (section 5). These reports almost certainly count recycled wood products (panels, chipboard feedstock) and new products made from reclaimed wood. Cite them only as an example of unreliable estimates.
- **The FMI USD 1.0 bn (2025) figure** for European component reuse is the closest in scope to Tallyard. It is plausible in order of magnitude given the bottom-up check below, but it has no visible method. Interpolating its CAGR gives about USD 1.8 bn (about EUR 1.6 bn, GBP 1.35 bn) in 2030 (1.0 x 1.122^5; my arithmetic).
- **Bottom-up cross-check (rough; every step is an assumption to be replaced with better data):**
  - France reused about 240 kt of building products in 2024, and its reuse sector employs about 1,600 FTE (ADEME, section 5).
  - At Rotor DC's roughly EUR 83,000 turnover per FTE, the French sector would turn over about EUR 130 m (about GBP 115 m). Many French operators are social economy structures with lower revenue per head, so EUR 80 m to 130 m is a reasonable range. That implies about EUR 330 to 550 per tonne reused.
  - France is about 14% of EU27 construction investment. If every EU country reused at French intensity, EU reuse would be about 1.7 Mt and EUR 0.55 to 0.95 bn.
  - France leads on policy, so actual EU reuse today is probably lower: roughly EUR 0.3 to 0.7 bn of reclaimed material sales a year, about GBP 0.26 to 0.6 bn.
  - At the REP PMCB 5% reuse target applied EU-wide (10 to 20 times today's 0.2% to 0.5%), the potential is in the low tens of billions of euros. Treat that as a theoretical ceiling, not a forecast.
  - Some of the French 240 kt is reused on the same site and never goes through a marketplace, so the share Tallyard could address is smaller than these totals.
- Tallyard's 8% commission on EUR 0.3 to 0.7 bn of EU-wide transactions gives a theoretical commission pool of EUR 24 to 56 m a year if Tallyard handled all of it. This is a TAM-style ceiling for the report writer to refine.

### Gaps
- Circle Economy (Circularity Gap reports), the Ellen MacArthur Foundation, EIT (Climate-KIC, RawMaterials), Dutch studies (ABN AMRO, Metabolic, Copper8, TNO) and Danish or Belgian sector valuations were not reached because the search budget ran out. These may hold better-founded European estimates.
- The FCRBE socio-economic survey (turnover and jobs of NWE reclamation dealers) exists but its figures were not retrieved; it is the best candidate for a sourced bottom-up base. See [Opalis documentation](https://opalis.eu/en/documentation).
- No estimate was found for a "construction materials marketplace" (B2B online) in Europe.
- An Accenture figure of EUR 1.8 bn of circular economy benefits by 2030, quoted by a market research site, could not be verified and is not construction-specific; exclude it.

## 5. The French reuse market: volumes, value, projects and growth since PEMD and REP PMCB

### Takeaway
France reused about 240,000 t of building products and materials in 2024, under 1% of the market. The sector has 385 physical sites, 10 digital platforms and about 1,600 FTE (ADEME, 2026). Reuse tracked by the REP PMCB scheme is much lower: 29.3 kt in 2024, under 0.3% of 10.7 Mt collected, against targets of 2% to 5%. Eco-organisation budgets are rising fast (EUR 121 m in 2023, EUR 170 m in 2024, about EUR 610 m projected for 2027) and part of them funds reuse. No euro value of the French reuse market has been published.

### Inferences
- Two French volume figures exist and differ by a factor of eight: 240 kt (ADEME, whole market including on-site reuse) and 29.3 kt (reuse tracked by the REP scheme). Use 240 kt for market sizing and 29.3 kt as evidence that the EPR channel is underperforming. Both put reuse at 0.3% to 0.5% of French building waste (46 Mt).
- Growth since PEMD (July 2023) and REP PMCB (2023) is not measurable from the sources found; no time series was retrieved. The rapid rise in eco-organisation budgets (EUR 121 m to a projected EUR 610 m in four years) and the 2027 reform suggest a funding pool. Tallyard could pursue it in France through reuse subsidies, the logistics and storage support the ESS France study points to, and PEMD data flows.
- The French sector is fragmented: 385 sites averaging about 4 FTE each (1,600 / 385), with only 10 digital players. That supports a marketplace and aggregation thesis, but competition from existing French platforms (not named in the sources found) needs a separate competitor scan.

### Gaps
- No euro turnover figure for the French reuse sector was found. ADEME's 2026 page may contain one; it was not readable in this session.
- No count of PEMD diagnostics filed since July 2023 (the CSTB platform) was found; that would give the yearly flow of large donor buildings in France.
- No Booster du Reemploi results for 2024 or 2025 were found.
- The source behind the 29.3 kt figure (probably the OCAB or ADEME REP PMCB annual report) was not identified.

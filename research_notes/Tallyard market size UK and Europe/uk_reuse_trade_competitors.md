# UK reclaimed construction materials: trade size today and the competitor map

Method note for the report writer: every finding below comes from web search result extracts gathered on 17 October 2026. Direct page fetches failed for every domain tried (DNS errors from the fetch tool: decomag.co.uk, asbp.org.uk, ukgbc.org, newcivilengineer.com, hts.uk.com, london.gov.uk, relondon.gov.uk, salvoweb.com, Companies House), and the shared web search budget ran out before the last batch of queries (ceiling tile reuse firms, timber reuse, NFDC market size, audit fees, Salvo dealer pricing, newest steel reuse news). Figures quoted from an extract were not re-read on the source page, so treat single-source numbers with care. Where a source gives no date, that is stated. "Estimate" means a third-party or vendor estimate; "filed" means drawn from Companies House accounts (via a summariser such as Endole or Pappers).

## 1. What is the UK architectural salvage and reclamation trade worth per year, and how many dealers are there?

### Takeaway
There is no recent, survey-based figure. The only headline number is Salvo founder Thornton Kay's estimate of about £300m a year (about 1% of UK building materials spend, said to be flat since the 1990s), and Salvo puts UK and Irish dealer stock at about 150,000 tonnes. The last full trade surveys were 1998 and 2007 (Salvo with BRE, and the CRWP "BigREc" survey). No dealer census was found.

### Cited Findings
- Value: Thornton Kay (Salvo founder) said architectural salvage sold in the UK "has barely changed since the 1990s", at about 1% of money spent in the building materials sector, "around £300 million a year". Interview estimate, not a survey; article date not shown in the extract (search tool described it as over a year old). - [Decomag](https://decomag.co.uk/content/salvage-and-reclamation-its-womans-world)
- Share: Salvo's estimate that only about 1% of building materials come from reclaimed sources is repeated in secondary guides, with a WRAP-sourced figure of about 1% as well (dates not shown). - [GreenSpec](http://www.greenspec.co.uk/building-design/reclaimed-materials/); [Green Building Encyclopaedia](https://greenbuildingencyclopaedia.uk/uncategorized/salvo-architectural-salvage-link/)
- Stock: "UK and Irish reclamation stockholders have around 150,000 tonnes of stock available for immediate reuse, but most projects reuse none" (Salvo article, date not shown in extract). - [SalvoWEB, Making the case for more reuse](https://www.salvoweb.com/salvonews/41763-making-the-case-for-more-reuse)
- Benchmark: the Salvo Reclamation Protocol (1990s) used a UK average of 2% "brought-in reclaimed material by value, volume, or mass" for a new building, rising to 5% for green exemplars. - [SalvoWEB, Making the case for more reuse](https://www.salvoweb.com/salvonews/41763-making-the-case-for-more-reuse)
- Historic surveys: Salvo surveys of UK salvage businesses in 1998 and 2007 showed "a large increase in the value of sales but a general decrease in the volumes of materials salvaged" as dealers sold more new and reproduction stock. The 1998 survey was the first complete survey of the UK reclaimed building materials sector, run by Salvo under BRE supervision. - [Building Conservation](https://www.buildingconservation.com/articles/salvage-sustainability/salvage-sustainability.htm); [FutuREuse, BigREc survey 1998](https://futureuse.co.uk/resources/bigrec-survey-1998/)
- A 2024 peer-reviewed paper on UK timber salvage cites the 2007 CRWP BigREc survey and reports that the amount of wood reclaimed "has decreased significantly in the last decades". - [ScienceDirect, Journal of Cleaner Production 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959652624040782)
- Bricks (low reliability, a Salvo article built from AI-generated reports): about 2.5 billion bricks arise as UK demolition waste each year, of which about 5% (about 125 million) are reclaimed for reuse; the other 95% are crushed for fill or aggregate. Reclaimed wood volumes "are limited due to inconsistent reporting". - [SalvoWEB, AI reports on reclaimed building materials reuse](https://www.salvoweb.com/salvonews/40976-ai-reports-on-reclaimed-building-materials-reuse)
- Fragmentation: a reclaimed materials retailer says the market is "highly fragmented" and "widely accepted" to be worth "hundreds of millions of pounds annually", with no source or year. - [The Reclaimed Company](https://shop.thereclaimedcompany.co.uk/home/architectural-salvage-uk-the-market-opportunity/)
- Dealer count: no census found. A 2026 curated directory lists 21 UK yards, showrooms and dealers and notes the trade self-polices through the Salvo Code (a curated list, not a count). Salvo runs the main directory and marketplace. - [Wholemood, checked August 2026](https://wholemood.com/reclamation-yards-uk/); [SalvoWEB](https://www.salvoweb.com/)
- Waste context (volume ceiling): Defra estimates the UK generated 59.4 Mt of non-hazardous construction and demolition (C&D) waste in 2020 and recovered 55.0 Mt; England alone generated 63.0 Mt in 2022 and recovered 59.4 Mt (about 94%). The recovery rate counts backfilling, which inflates it. - [Wikiwaste, C&D waste](https://wikiwaste.org.uk/index.php/Construction_and_Demolition_Waste); [Defra, UK statistics on waste](https://www.data.gov.uk/dataset/882186e7-97b0-4ad0-b253-e28607252f42/uk_statistics_on_waste)
- London context: the built environment produces more than 50% of London's waste (ReLondon, May 2021). The Material Reuse Portal says the built environment "consumes 400 million tonnes of materials every year, producing 54% of all waste in the capital" (UK-wide tonnage, London waste share; date not shown). - [ReLondon, Sourcing reclaimed construction materials, 2021](https://relondon.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/ReLondon-Sourcing-reclaimed-construction-materials-May-2021.pdf); [Material Reuse Portal](https://materialreuseportal.com/)
- ReLondon's London Circular Construction Coalition paper says reuse "remains a niche topic due to the lack of incentives and infrastructure to boost supply and demand for secondary materials" (date not shown). - [ReLondon](https://relondon.gov.uk/resources/why-we-need-a-london-circular-construction-coalition)

### Inferences
- If £300m is about 1% of UK building materials spend, Salvo is implicitly sizing UK materials spend at about £30bn a year. The writer should treat £300m as an order-of-magnitude figure, last anchored to surveys from 1998 and 2007.
- The £300m "architectural salvage" figure is mostly heritage and domestic product (bricks, flooring, fireplaces, doors, stone, timber) sold by yards. Commercial donor-building materials (structural steel, raised access floor, ceiling tiles, glazing, partitions) mostly move outside the Salvo dealer base, through stockholders such as Cleveland Steel and Tubes, specialist flooring resellers and brokers. Tallyard's addressable trade today is therefore a small slice of £300m plus a separate, much smaller commercial strip-out reuse flow.
- Volume ceiling: about 150,000 t of dealer stock against about 60 Mt a year of C&D waste means stock on hand is about 0.25% of annual C&D arisings (my arithmetic). Even the full UK C&D stream is mostly hardcore and soil, not reusable components.
- Rough brick value (my arithmetic, low confidence because the 125m figure comes from an AI-generated Salvo article): 125m bricks at the £550 to £1,800 per thousand reclaimed price range in section 3 gives about £70m to £225m a year at retail.

### Gaps
- No Salvo, BRE, Bioregional or ReLondon survey of trade value newer than 2007 was found. No dated source for the £300m figure.
- No count of UK reclamation dealers or employment. ONS business counts for the relevant SIC codes were not searched (budget).
- No figure for the value of commercial strip-out reuse (as opposed to heritage salvage).

## 2. Reused structural steel in the UK: tonnes traded, players, prices

### Takeaway
UK structural steel reuse is a supply-constrained niche with effectively one active stockholder since EMR exited in early 2025: Cleveland Steel and Tubes (CST), holding about 6,000 to 7,000 t of reclaimed structural sections. Flagship London projects use hundreds of tonnes each, with most of the steel taken from donor buildings on or near site. No national tonnage figure exists; the evidence points to low thousands of tonnes a year. No 2024 to 2026 per-tonne price for reclaimed sections was found; the main benchmark is SCI's historic £200 to £520 a tonne gap between new and scrap.

### Cited Findings
- Market structure: EMR (European Metal Recycling) "exited the market in the early part of 2025, leaving just one supplier of reused steel currently active in the market; Cleveland Steel & Tubes". CST "holds over 7000 tonnes of reclaimed steel available for reuse", including much of the steel EMR had acquired. - [HTS, The future of steel reuse](https://hts.uk.com/news-views/the-future-of-steel-reuse/)
- EMR Reusable Steels had launched in 2023 (some coverage says 2022), opened a Coventry site, aimed for a national network by 2030, claimed the first reusable steel with an EPD, and won Recycled Product of the Year at the 2024 National Recycling Awards. - [EMR](https://uk.emrgroup.com/find-out-more/latest-news/reusable-steels-wins-national-recycling-award); [Packaging Europe](https://packagingeurope.com/news/emr-reusable-steels-targets-carbon-reduction-with-reusable-material/12214.article)
- CST stock: about 100,000+ t of tubular stock plus 6,000 t of structural sections (sponsor profile, date not shown); total stock about 84,000 to 85,000 t, mostly tubes recovered from steel mills and oil and gas (Q1 2024 stocklist). - [Circular Steel](https://circularsteel.com/sponsors/cleveland-steel-tubes/); [CST Q1 2024 stocklist](https://cleveland-steel.com/sites/default/files/2024-01/cst-q1-2024-stocklist-international.pdf); [CST stock page](https://cleveland-steel.com/our-range-steel-tube-stock)
- CST financials (filed, year to 31 Dec 2023, via Endole): turnover not reported (small-company filing), 49 employees, total assets £1.89m, net assets about £0.59m. Third-party revenue estimates of $11.2m to $14.1m are not from accounts. Companies House number 01344027; latest accounts made up to 31 Dec 2025 (figures not seen). CST is part of the Bianco Group. - [Endole](https://open.endole.co.uk/insight/company/01344027-cleveland-steel-tubes-limited); [Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/01344027); [ZoomInfo estimate](https://www.zoominfo.com/c/cleveland-steel--tubes-ltd/22793856)
- Supply problem: demolition contractors "are not properly incentivised to recover steel", causing stop-start supply (January 2025). - [New Civil Engineer, 31 Jan 2025](https://www.newcivilengineer.com/in-depth/why-is-steel-reuse-uptake-low-despite-proven-benefits-31-01-2025/)
- 30 Duke Street St James's (GPE; Mace; Elliott Wood; Make; completed about September 2026), described as the UK's largest commercial office steel reuse project: about 78% of the frame reclaimed, about 440 t; 67% of that came from donor buildings formerly on the site, the rest from EMR and Cleveland Steel stock. Build News: 580 t used on site, 439 t reclaimed, mainly from City Place House. GPE: 450 t from another GPE building, and 400 t "returned to the market for future use". Carbon saving about 744 t. - [Elliott Wood](https://www.elliottwood.co.uk/latest/180-piccadilly-londons-largest-steel-reuse-project); [Build News](https://www.buildnews.co.uk/30-duke-street-tops-out-as-londons-largest-commercial-reused-steel-project/); [GPE via DirectorsTalk](https://www.directorstalkinterviews.com/great-portland-estates-completes-fully-pre-let-30-duke-street-st-james-s/4121261673); [Mace](https://www.macegroup.com/en-us/news/mace-construct-completes-30-duke-street-st-jamess-a-landmark-68000-sq-ft-commercial-development/)
- Landsec Print/Ink hybrid (Mace): 120 t of reused steelwork from CST, fabricated into about 500 beams (date not shown). - [Construction Management](https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/mace-landsec-low-carbon-hybrid/)
- Grosvenor Holbein Gardens retrofit (Belgravia): 9 t reclaimed from a nearby demolition plus 15 t of reused steel from Cleveland Steel, which also checked surface condition, coatings and end preparation. - [Grosvenor](https://www.grosvenor.com/news-insights/some-of-uk%E2%80%99s-first-salvaged-steelwork-reused-in-holbein-gardens-retrofit)
- Landsec Timber Square: floors include a frame of reused steel. - [London Property Alliance, Retrofit first, Jan 2023](https://www.londonpropertyalliance.com/wp-content/uploads/WPA_Retrofit-first_310123-002.pdf)
- Price gap (historic): SCI found the difference between new structural steel and scrap averaged £280/t over 2001 to 2016, never below £200/t and up to about £520/t. Added reuse costs suggested: deconstruction about 75% of erection cost and about 20% extra on fabrication, highly project dependent. - [SCI report via ASBP, 2016](https://asbp.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Barriers-to-structural-steel-reuse-and-means-of-overcoming-them-SCI-Report-1.pdf)
- SCI's reuse protocol puts the long-term new-versus-scrap difference at over £300/t, framed as the potential saving from reuse; testing is priced per piece, not per tonne. - [SCI, Protocol for reusing structural steel](https://steel-sci.com/assets/downloads/steel-reuse-protocol-v06.pdf); [PBC Today](https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/building-control-news/reusing-structural-steel/62082/)
- Vendor claim: CST says developers can save "up to 60%" against new steel on small residential jobs, including extra engineer fees (date not shown). - [Builders Merchants News](https://www.buildersmerchantsnews.co.uk/news/product-news/savings-residential-reused-steel-revealed-renovators)
- New steel: British Steel raised structural section transaction prices by £50/t for new orders from 14 November 2025. - [Construction News, 27 Nov 2025](https://www.constructionnews.co.uk/supply-chain/british-steel-hikes-steel-price-by-50-per-tonne-27-11-2025/)
- New steel (low confidence, unsourced calculator): UB and UC sections about £1,100 to £1,200/t ex works in early 2026. - [Mixdesigncalc](https://mixdesigncalc.com/steel-price-calculator/)
- Fabricated frame: BCSA Costing Steelwork (September 2026) frame rates imply roughly £3,100 to £4,300 per tonne for a fabricated frame (derived by a builder's guide). - [MSB Group](https://www.msbgroupuk.com/news/steel-frame-building-cost-uk)
- Scrap floor: UK No. 1 old steel scrap ranged about £105 to £210/t through 2025; plate and structural scrap about £180 to £320/t (estimate). - [letsrecycle.com, 2025](https://www.letsrecycle.com/prices/metals/ferrous-metal-prices/ferrous-scrap-metal-prices-2025/); [steelprices.co.uk](https://steelprices.co.uk/steel-recycling)
- Other initiatives: ASBP-led DISRUPT (Delivering Innovative Steel ReUse ProjecT) released a toolkit to find reusable steel in existing structures; ReLondon case study on Channel Projects' steel reuse plans. - [IOM3](https://www.iom3.org/resource/disrupting-steel-reuse.html); [ReLondon](https://relondon.gov.uk/resources/case-study-channel-projects-ironing-out-plans-for-structural-steel-reuse)

### Inferences
- UK traded reclaimed structural steel is likely in the low thousands of tonnes a year: one stockholder with about 6,000 to 7,000 t of sections on hand, and the largest project ever at about 440 t, two thirds of it donor steel that never entered the traded market. This is an inference; no source gives a national figure.
- Illustrative value (my arithmetic, inputs unverified): 2,000 to 7,000 t at a discount to a new mill price of about £1,100 to £1,200/t would be a merchant-level trade of very roughly £2m to £8m a year.
- Commission illustration for Tallyard (inputs unverified): a 440 t donor steel package priced at, say, £700 to £1,000/t is about £300k to £440k of material; 8% would be about £25k to £35k per flagship deal. The GPE "400 t returned to the market" shows donor owners already generate surplus that needs a buyer.
- With EMR gone, CST is both the obvious partner (stock, testing know-how) and the incumbent Tallyard competes with for donor steel.

### Gaps
- No 2024 to 2026 per-tonne price for reclaimed sections from CST or anyone else.
- No UK tonnage of reused structural steel per year; no BCSA or SCI figure found. UK annual structural steel consumption not found (search budget).
- "Steel Reuse Network" and "Reused Steel" as named entities were not found. No newer 2026 entrants found before the budget ran out.

## 3. Raised access floors, ceiling tiles, glazing, bricks, stone and timber: volumes, values, players, margins

### Takeaway
Raised access floor (RAF) is the most developed commercial reuse flow: a manufacturer takeback (Tate), several specialist resellers, and reclaimed panels trading at about £21 to £38/m² against about £25 to £70/m² new. Ceiling tiles are mostly recycled (Armstrong closed loop), not reused. Glazing reuse is rare and project-specific. Bricks have a mature merchant trade at £550 to £2,400 per thousand. Stone reuse appears as large single-project lots. No national volumes or margins were found for any family.

### Cited Findings
#### Raised access floors
- Tate runs a reuse takeback that has recovered "tens of thousands of square metres" of panels from UK projects; its Reuse Panel is a steel-encapsulated wood core panel reclaimed from earlier installations (date not shown). - [Tate, reuse](https://www.tateglobal.com/emea/reuse/); [Tate Reuse Panel](https://www.tateglobal.com/emea/products/raised-access-floors/reuse-panel/)
- 40 Grosvenor Place (Belgravia): 1,250 m² of Tate Reuse panels. - [Tate case study](https://www.tateglobal.com/emea/insights-resources/case-studies/40-grosvenor-place-uk/)
- Mace, 60 Sloane Avenue: 4,000 m² of existing RAF reused after strip-out plus 4,000 m² of reclaimed tiles from other sites; about 400 t CO2 saved (date not shown). - [Mace](https://www.macegroup.com/case-studies/reclaimed-raised-access-flooring/)
- Landsec Timber Square: c.33,450 m² of floor tiles reclaimed from other projects, about 1,362 t CO2e saved; the area looks large against a 35,600 m² scheme, so check before use. - [UKGBC, Timber Square](https://ukgbc.org/resources/timber-square/)
- Players: Raised Access Floor Installations Ltd claims to be "the UK's largest supplier of recycle and second user access flooring"; Recycle Access Floors Ltd buys used panels and says all are "100% repurposed"; Circular Building Solutions offers reuse services; Access Floors Distribution resells recycled panels. - [Raised Access Floor Installations](https://raisedaccessfloor.co.uk/enviro-range/); [Recycle Access Floors](https://recycleaccessfloors.co.uk/); [Circular Building Solutions](https://circularbuildingsolutions.co.uk/reuse-services/)
- Prices (asking, 2025 to 2026, ex delivery): Access Floors Distribution sells recycled Tate/Kingspan RMG600 or RG3 31 mm panels at £8.90 and £7.66 ex VAT, ungraded and uncleaned; Material Index lists reclaimed Tate/Kingspan 600 x 600 x 31 mm panels at £10.86 to £13.53 a piece; eBay about £7.50 a panel. - [Access Floors Distribution](https://accessfloorsdistribution.co.uk/product/recycled-raised-access-floor-panels-31mm/); [Material Index, RAF category](https://material-index.co.uk/categories/raised-access-flooring); [Material Index, panels](https://material-index.co.uk/products/raised-floor-panels); [eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/b/bn_7023590699)
- New RAF benchmark (undated guide): £25 to £45/m² for chipboard or wood-core panels, £40 to £70/m² for cementitious steel panels. - [CCL Interiors](https://ccl-interiors.com/blog/how-much-does-raised-access-flooring-cost-in-the-uk/)
- Kingspan warned in 2015 against contractors lifting and reusing panels from strip-outs (performance risk). - [The Construction Index, 2015](https://www.theconstructionindex.co.uk/news/view/kingspan-warns-against-reusing-flooring-panels)

#### Ceiling tiles
- Armstrong's UK and Ireland scheme has run since 2003; all UK mineral tile recycling takes place at its Team Valley (Gateshead) plant; the 2020 relaunch offers free collection via the local distributor, with tiles recycled into new tiles. One source says collection is free when 2,000 m² of acceptable tiles are collected. Global programme totals at relaunch: 15 million m² and 61,000 t diverted. - [Building Centre, 2020](https://www.buildingcentre.co.uk/news/articles/armstrong-ceiling-solutions-has-simplified-its-recycling-scheme); [Building Centre PDF](https://buildingcentre.co.uk/media/_file/pdf/22430_pdf42.pdf); [Construction Update, April 2020](https://construction-update.co.uk/2020/04/08/armstrong-ceiling-solutions-has-simplified-its-recycling-scheme-2/)
- Project scale: Queen Elizabeth University Hospital, Glasgow, over 10,000 m² or 35 t of off-cuts recycled. - [RIBAJ](https://www.ribaj.com/spec/armstrong-ceilings-prove-just-the-tonic-for-patients-and-the-environment-in-glasgow/)

#### Glazing
- 1 Triton Square, London (Arup, about 2021): over 3,500 m² of facade glass panels taken down, cleaned, regasketed and reinstalled, about 2,400 t CO2 avoided. Arup notes heat treatment makes cutting and reforming glass "difficult or impossible" and coatings degrade. - [Arup](https://www.arup.com/en-us/insights/what-reclaiming-facade-materials-teaches-us-about-sustainable-buildings/)
- Guardian Glass: about 45 t of glazing from Mountbatten House, Basingstoke, recycled into float glass (recycling, not reuse). - [Guardian Glass](https://www.guardianglass.com/eu/en/who-we-are/stewardship/environmental-stewardship/life-cycle-of-glass/end-of-life/retrofitting-and-facade-recycling)
- Excess Materials Exchange cites three London examples of glass partition reuse. - [ASBP, EME case study](https://asbp.org.uk/case-studies/eme)

#### Bricks
- 2025 to 2026 price guides: reclaimed red common £550 to £950 per thousand, Victorian stock £800 to £1,600, London stock £1,050 to £1,800; handmade red (cleaned, graded) £800 to £1,200 and reclaimed London yellow stock £1,200 to £2,400 per thousand (VAT basis varies; which guide gives which range was not confirmed). - [Brickseeker, 2026](https://www.brickseeker.co.uk/blog/how-much-do-bricks-cost-uk-2026); [Imperial Bricks, 2025](https://www.imperialbricks.co.uk/guidance/cost-of-bricks-in-the-uk-2025/)
- Merchant listings: Dorton Reclaim, reclaimed red £900 per thousand plus VAT and others £750 per thousand; Watling Reclamation, Preston red £1,400 per thousand; Cawarden, most reclaimed bricks £1.20 to £2.40 each including VAT. - [Dorton Reclaim](https://www.dortonreclaim.com/product-category/paving-building/bricks/); [Watling Reclamation](https://watlingreclamation.co.uk/product-category/building-materials/bricks-reclaimed-new/); [Cawarden](https://cawardenreclaim.co.uk/reclaimed-bricks-buying-guide/)
- Volume: about 125 million bricks a year reclaimed out of about 2.5 billion in demolition waste (low reliability, see section 1). London Reclaimed Brick Merchants is listed on ReLondon's portal. - [SalvoWEB](https://www.salvoweb.com/salvonews/40976-ai-reports-on-reclaimed-building-materials-reuse); [Material Reuse Portal, suppliers](https://materialreuseportal.com/Reclaim-Salvage-Suppliers)

#### Stone
- Grosvenor, 56 Davies Street (Mayfair): 4,750 blocks of Portland stone, 550 t, disassembled to be reconditioned and reused (date not shown; extract attributed to Grosvenor news). - [Grosvenor](https://www.grosvenor.com/news-insights/one-of-london%E2%80%99s-most-sustainable-office-developments-gets-approval)
- Material Index is profiled in the stone trade press for brokering reclaimed stone. - [Stone Specialist](https://www.stonespecialist.com/news/profile-material-index)

#### Timber
- UK reclaimed timber volumes have fallen over recent decades (2024 paper citing the 2007 BigREc survey); no current tonnage found. - [ScienceDirect 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959652624040782)

### Inferences
- Reclaimed RAF sells at roughly £21 to £38/m² (my conversion: 600 x 600 mm panel is 0.36 m²), close to or below new chipboard panels, so the discount to new is thin. Reseller margin is the spread between the price paid to strip-out contractors (not found) and these asking prices.
- RAF and Portland stone show that single donor buildings yield lots of thousands of m² or hundreds of tonnes, which suits a listing and matching marketplace.
- Ceiling tiles compete with a free manufacturer takeback, so the reuse value is weak unless tiles are reinstalled as is.

### Gaps
- No national volumes (m², tonnes) or market values for any family.
- No typical margins for reclamation merchants or RAF resellers. Encore Environmental and named ceiling tile reuse firms were not found (search budget).
- No data on stone cladding or timber reuse firms beyond single projects.

## 4. UK digital platforms, marketplaces and circularity software: the competitor and comparable map

### Takeaway
The UK field is small, early and lightly funded. Every UK-native player found has raised under about £1m (Globechain about $1m; Circuland €750k; Material Index undisclosed pre-seed; Enviromate self-funded). Models split three ways: listing and subscription marketplaces (Globechain, Enviromate, Salvo), audit-led brokers with a marketplace (Material Index, Reusefully, Maconda), and passport or SaaS tools (Madaster, Circuland, ROMULUS on Upcyclea). Public aggregators (ReLondon's Material Reuse Portal, Enfield's EME instance) are free. No UK player publishes a commission rate or GMV.

### Cited Findings
#### Globechain (London; B2B reuse marketplace across sectors including construction)
- Model: corporate members pay to list, charities and individuals join free; pay-as-you-go about €11 (about £10) per upload, an annual subscription for unlimited listings, or bespoke deals; large corporates pay premium annual memberships. - [Watt](https://www.watt.co.uk/online-reuse-marketplace-aids-companies-achieve-sustainability-goals-cut-costs/); [Cluster Collaboration](http://www.clustercollaboration.eu/content/online-reuse-platform-globechain)
- Traction (self-reported, dates not shown): over 10,000 members; clients named include Chanel, H&M, Radisson, B&Q, ISG Construction, IHG and the NHS. Partner Axil says the network has reused over 5 million items, diverted 65,000 t and returned over £400m in disposal savings. - [UKGBC, ESG reuse marketplace](https://ukgbc.org/resources/esg-reuse-marketplace/); [Axil](https://axil-is.com/press-releases/axil-integrated-services-launches-a-surplus-asset-reuse-marketplace-in-partnership-with-globechain/)
- Funding: about $0.99m to $1.08m in total; £750k round listed April 2019 (others say 2018 seed); investors include Kindred Capital, Force Over Mass and Ascension. - [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/globechain); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/233208-82); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/globechain-1)
- Revenue: GetLatka estimates $4m in 2022 and $1.6m in 2021 (estimate, unverified). Watt says it broke even in year three. - [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/globechain); [Watt](https://www.watt.co.uk/online-reuse-marketplace-aids-companies-achieve-sustainability-goals-cut-costs/)
- Filed: Globechain (UK) Limited, company 09393158, last accounts to 31 August 2025; 4 employees per Pappers. - [Pappers](https://www.pappers.co.uk/company/globechain-uk-limited-09393158); [Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/09393158)

#### Enviromate (surplus building materials marketplace)
- Founded about 2015 (sources say 2014 to 2016) by Reiss Salustro-Pilson and Nigel Van Wassenhoven; buy, sell and donate leftover materials; no commission or selling fees; four tiers (Free, Premium, Premium+, Wholesalers); self-funded; over 15,000 members at relaunch; claimed diversion 4,000 t to 50,000 t depending on source. Users are mainly trades and DIY. - [PR Newswire, relaunch](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/multi-award-winning-marketplace-start-up-enviromate-relaunches-617153613.html); [Upcyclist, 2018](https://www.upcyclist.co.uk/2018/06/marketplace-for-used-building-materials/); [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/enviromate-reuse-limited)

#### Material Index (London; audits, brokerage and marketplace; the closest UK analogue to Tallyard)
- Services: pre-demolition and pre-redevelopment audits, reuse consultancy, brokerage and a B2B marketplace for reclaimed, refurbished and excess materials; over 300 trade partners (manufacturers and resellers who collect, sort, store and recertify); UK-wide audits with local teams; audit fees quoted per project from site address, floor area and programme. - [Material Index](https://material-index.co.uk/); [Material Index audits](https://material-index.co.uk/audits); [Material Index PRDA](https://material-index.co.uk/audits/pre-redevelopment-audit)

- Funding: pre-seed led by Clearance Venture Partners with Zinc (amount and date undisclosed); later UKRI funding for an AI model; UK patent pending. - [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/material-index); [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/material-index); [Digital Catapult](https://www.digicatapult.org.uk/case-studies/study/material-index-uses-ai-to-boost-constructions-circular-economy/)
- Traction: UKGBC case study, coordinated sale of 23 material types through 8 reuse partners for total sales of just over £17,000 (date not shown); partnership with fit-out firm Oktra. - [UKGBC](https://ukgbc.org/resources/reused-materials-exchange-marketplace/); [Construction Management](https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/oktra-teams-up-with-material-index-to-turn-sea-of-waste-into-new-materials/)

#### Circuland (London; digital material passports, stock database, reuse marketplace)

- Funding: pre-seed €750k (also reported as £620k) led by Apeiron Ventures with Astylab Ventures; CB Insights total $810k. - [Apeiron](https://apeiron.vc/temporibus-autem-quibusdam-et-aut-officiis-debitis-aut-rerum-neces-sitatibus-2/); [Startups of London](https://startupsoflondon.com/circuland-secures-investment-to-revolutionise-circular-tech/); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/circuland)

### Inferences
- Funding gap: UK reuse platforms have raised roughly £0.6m to £1m each; the best-funded adjacent marketplace (Materials Market, new materials) raised £2m. A Tallyard seed round of a few million pounds would make it the best-funded UK reuse marketplace on this evidence.
- Material Index is the direct competitor (audit-led supply capture plus marketplace plus licensing). Reusefully plus Maconda plus ROMULUS form a second audit-to-passport alliance with City of London backing. Globechain and Enviromate are horizontal or small-trade marketplaces that do not handle structural or specified materials.
- Disclosed traction is small: £17k of sales across 23 material types on a UKGBC showcase project, and Globechain at 4 employees in filed data, which conflicts with GetLatka's $4m revenue estimate (likely overstated).
- Madaster's UK list prices (£3,500 to £15,000 a year) bracket Tallyard's placeholder subscriptions (£1,200 to £4,800), which supports them as plausible.

### Gaps
- Kiaro, The Reuse Hub or Waste4Change, Cirkla, Concular UK and a "Loop" UK platform: nothing found, or not searched before the budget ran out. Safi (formerly TrueCircle) appeared in a directory with $25m funding, unverified and focused on waste processing, not construction.
- No filed turnover for Globechain, Enviromate, Material Index, Circuland or Maconda (Companies House could not be opened).
- No commission rate, take rate or GMV published by any UK reuse marketplace.

## 5. Pre-demolition audit and circular economy consulting market in the UK

### Takeaway
Demand is policy-driven: London Plan Circular Economy Statements for GLA-referable schemes expect pre-redevelopment and pre-demolition audits, and BREEAM has required pre-demolition audits since 2011. Providers include Material Index, Reusefully, Maconda, Collect Eco and engineering or sustainability consultancies. No published fee per audit and no confirmed annual count were found.

### Cited Findings
- London Plan Guidance (March 2022): a pre-demolition audit is a detailed inventory of materials to be managed on demolition; applicants are "strongly encouraged" to submit an independent pre-demolition audit with all applications that involve demolition, which can sometimes be secured by condition. Circular Economy Statements should include a pre-redevelopment audit, a pre-demolition audit and an operational waste plan; third-party verified pre-redevelopment audits are strongly encouraged. - [GLA, CES guidance PDF](https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/circular_economy_statements_lpg.pdf); [GLA, CES guidance page](https://www.london.gov.uk/programmes-strategies/planning/implementing-london-plan/london-plan-guidance/circular-economy-statement-guidance)
- GLA Circular Economy Monitoring 2023 (published November 2024) has a pre-redevelopment audit spotlight; the extract cut off at "There were 93", possibly the number of referable applications determined in 2023 (unconfirmed). A 2022 edition (published June 2024) has a pre-demolition audit section. - [GLA monitoring 2023](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/107727/download); [GLA monitoring 2022](https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-06/Circular-Economy-Monitoring-2022-FINAL-June-2024.pdf)
- BREEAM 2011 New Construction requires a pre-demolition audit; BRE advises doing it well before demolition so reclaimed materials can find homes (2013). - [The Construction Index, 2013](https://www.theconstructionindex.co.uk/news/view/the-case-for-pre-demolition-audits)
- Tender practice: a conference paper recommends tenders include a pre-demolition audit, a reclamation valuation and environmental quantification, so audits are often bundled into demolition contracts rather than procured alone. - [CIB paper, Valuing the pre-demolition audit process](https://www.irbnet.de/daten/iconda/CIB864.pdf)
- Providers: Material Index (UK-wide, fee proposal after site address, floor area and programme); Reusefully; Syntegra; Material Reuse Portal lists further audit and deconstruction providers; UKGBC publishes a free pre-demolition audit tool. - [Material Index](https://material-index.co.uk/audits); [Reusefully](https://www.reusefully.co.uk/pre-demolition-audits); [Syntegra](https://syntegragroup.com/m-and-e2/services/circular-economy/); [Material Reuse Portal](https://www.materialreuseportal.com/Pre-Demolition-Audits); [UKGBC tool](https://ukgbc.org/resources/pre-demolition-audit-tool/)
- Other London policy: the City of London Corporation has a Circular Economy Framework and sponsors ROMULUS. - [City of London](https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/assets/Services-Environment/circular-economy-framework.pdf); [NLA](https://nla.london/news/romulus-a-new-circular-economy-initiative)

### Inferences
- If roughly 90 to 100 GLA-referable applications a year carry a Circular Economy Statement (unconfirmed reading of the 2023 report), and a large share involve existing buildings, London's mandatory-grade audit volume is on the order of tens to low hundreds a year, plus voluntary BREEAM audits and borough-level schemes. This is an inference.
- Audits are the main supply-capture channel: whoever writes the audit learns what a donor building holds before demolition. Material Index and Reusefully use audits as the front door to brokerage, which is the same wedge Tallyard targets through pre-demolition surveyors.

### Gaps
- No published audit fee. Tender notices for stand-alone audits were not found. Searches for vendor price lists failed when the budget ran out.
- The exact number of pre-demolition and pre-redevelopment audits per year (GLA monitoring PDFs could not be opened).

## 6. Evidence of willingness to pay for reuse brokerage, material passports or circularity software

### Takeaway
Hard price points exist only for passport software (Madaster UK: £3,500 to £15,000 a year, producers £8,500) and listing marketplaces (Globechain about £10 per upload or annual membership; Enviromate freemium tiers, no commission). Public portals and manufacturer takebacks are free, which caps what can be charged for simple listing. Developers clearly pay for audits and reuse consultants, but fee levels and brokerage commission rates are not published.

### Cited Findings
- Passport SaaS list prices: Madaster UK £3,500, £7,500 and £15,000 a year (Starter, Medium, Premium); Producer Premium £8,500 a year; extra users £1,000 a year (current page, date not shown). - [Madaster UK](https://madaster.co.uk/pricing-2/); [Madaster UK producers](https://madaster.co.uk/packages-for-producers/)
- Marketplace listing fees: Globechain about €11 (about £10) per upload, or an annual subscription, with large corporates paying premium memberships. - [Cluster Collaboration](http://www.clustercollaboration.eu/content/online-reuse-platform-globechain); [Watt](https://www.watt.co.uk/online-reuse-marketplace-aids-companies-achieve-sustainability-goals-cut-costs/)
- Enviromate charges no commission or selling fees and sells tiered memberships. - [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/multi-award-winning-marketplace-start-up-enviromate-relaunches-617153613.html)
- Free alternatives: ReLondon's Material Reuse Portal is free; ROMULUS is sponsored by the City of London; Armstrong collects used tiles free (from about 2,000 m²). - [Material Reuse Portal](https://materialreuseportal.com/); [NLA](https://nla.london/news/romulus-a-new-circular-economy-initiative); [Construction Update, 2020](https://construction-update.co.uk/2020/04/08/armstrong-ceiling-solutions-has-simplified-its-recycling-scheme-2/)
- Developers paying for reuse services: Landsec engaged Collect Eco; British Land commissioned a detailed audit of 2-3 Finsbury Avenue; Apetito commissioned an audit via KLH Sustainability. - [Blackburn](https://www.blackburnltd.com/insights/sustainable-office-refurbishment-case-study-landsec/); [Reusefully projects](https://www.reusefully.co.uk/projects)
- Buyer-side savings: CST claims savings of up to 60% against new steel on small jobs; SCI's historic new-versus-scrap gap of £200 to £520/t is the value pool that reuse shares between seller, broker and buyer. - [Builders Merchants News](https://www.buildersmerchantsnews.co.uk/news/product-news/savings-residential-reused-steel-revealed-renovators); [SCI via ASBP, 2016](https://asbp.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Barriers-to-structural-steel-reuse-and-means-of-overcoming-them-SCI-Report-1.pdf)
- Transaction size: Material Index's UKGBC showcase sold 23 material types for about £17,000 in total. - [UKGBC](https://ukgbc.org/resources/reused-materials-exchange-marketplace/)

### Inferences
- Tallyard's placeholder subscriptions (supplier £4,800, buyer organisation £3,600, professional seat £1,200) sit inside the Madaster UK range and well above Globechain or Enviromate listing prices. That is defensible for developers and consultants who need audit, passport and planning evidence, but risky for small dealers who can list free on public portals.
- On non-steel lots, 8% commission yields little per project (8% of £17,000 is about £1,360, my arithmetic). Commission revenue will concentrate in structural steel, stone and large RAF lots; subscriptions and services (audits, testing referral, storage, transport) likely carry early revenue.
- Free public infrastructure in London (Material Reuse Portal, ROMULUS, Tipping Point East) both validates demand and anchors price expectations low for basic listing.

### Gaps
- No UK reuse broker or marketplace publishes a commission or take rate.
- No published fee for pre-demolition audits, reuse consultancy (for example Collect Eco) or material passport services beyond Madaster list prices.
- No survey of developer or architect willingness to pay was found.

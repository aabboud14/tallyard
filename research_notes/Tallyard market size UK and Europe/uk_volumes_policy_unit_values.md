## 1. How much C&D waste do the UK, England and London generate, and what share is reused rather than recycled?

### Takeaway
England generates about 63 Mt a year of non-hazardous construction and demolition (C&D) waste, excluding excavation (63.0 Mt in 2022; 63.3 Mt provisional for 2024), and about 95% is "recovered". Recovery is almost entirely recycling and downcycling into aggregate. Direct reuse of building materials is only about 2 to 6% (UKGBC; industry average about 4%), and for structural steel sections 5 to 13% depending on the source and year. No current, reliable London-wide C&D tonnage exists.

### Cited Findings
**UK and England, official series (Defra "UK statistics on waste")**
- UK, 2020 (latest UK-level C&D figure on the page): 59.4 Mt of non-hazardous C&D waste generated, of which 55.0 Mt was recovered (about 92.6%). Defra notes that the C&D recovery rate was not updated in the latest release. Official statistics. ([Defra, UK statistics on waste](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/uk-waste-data/uk-statistics-on-waste))
- England, 2022: 63.0 Mt of non-hazardous C&D waste generated and 59.4 Mt recovered, with a recovery rate stated as 93.4%. This comes from a wiki that reproduces Defra digest tables, not from Defra directly. **Conflict flag:** 59.4 / 63.0 works out to 94.3%, not 93.4%, so either a tonnage or the rate was transcribed wrongly. Check the Defra table. ([WikiWaste, C&D waste](https://wikiwaste.org.uk/CDW)); the dataset covers England 2010 to 2022 ([data.gov.uk, UK Statistics on Waste](https://www.data.gov.uk/dataset/882186e7-97b0-4ad0-b253-e28607252f42/uk_statistics_on_waste))
- England, 2024 (provisional, Defra publication updated 19 January 2026): 63.3 Mt of non-hazardous C&D waste generated and 60.3 Mt recovered, a recovery rate of 95.3%. Reported in secondary trade press citing Defra. ([Agg-Net, "Construction waste recovery rate reaches 95.3%"](https://www.agg-net.com/news/construction-waste-recovery-rate-reaches-953))
- England, 2024, including excavation and dredging: construction, demolition and excavation activities produced 118.2 Mt, 66% of England's total waste generation. Secondary report of Defra figures (search extract; attribution between the two pages not confirmed). ([Agg-Net](https://www.agg-net.com/news/construction-waste-recovery-rate-reaches-953); [Rent-a-Skip, UK construction waste report 2026](https://www.rent-a-skip.co.uk/blog/uk-construction-waste-report-2026))
- England, 2020: 53.9 Mt of non-hazardous C&D waste generated and 50.3 Mt recovered (search extract of the Defra series). ([Defra, UK statistics on waste](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/uk-waste-data/uk-statistics-on-waste))
- C&D tonnages are uncertain because many construction firms reuse material on site, which probably understates the true totals. ([WikiWaste](https://wikiwaste.org.uk/CDW))
- An older "about 91% recovery" figure in trade content is based on 2018 data, so it should not be used as a current rate. ([Biffa](https://biffa.co.uk/biffa-insights/construction-waste-recycling))

**Conflicting headline shares (definitions differ)**
- Holcim UK puts construction and demolition at about 32% of total UK waste by volume (2026), while a Reconomy article puts construction's share of all UK waste at 62%. The difference is almost certainly whether excavation soils are included. ([Holcim UK](https://www.holcim.co.uk/news-and-resources/press-releases/Building-it-Twice-Podcast); [Reconomy](https://www.reconomy.com/?p=33860))
- RIBA (2021): "every year 50,000 buildings are demolished in the UK, producing 126 million tonnes of waste, which represent two-thirds of the UK's total waste". This conflates all CD&E waste, including excavation, with demolition. It is advocacy, not an official statistic. ([Real Asset Insight reporting RIBA, July 2021](https://realassetinsight.com/2021/07/12/riba-demolitions-should-be-stopped-to-lower-emissions/))
- An academic source (data about 2013 to 2014) gives about 50,000 buildings demolished a year producing 45 Mt of C&D waste, mostly concrete, masonry, bricks and steel. ([ICE Engineering Sustainability, Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/jensu/article/172/3/119/428610))

**Reuse share (as opposed to recovery or recycling)**
- UKGBC: only about 2 to 6% of building materials are directly reused, with an industry-average reuse rate of about 4% (undated page, current in 2025 to 2026). ([UKGBC, Reused Materials Exchange Marketplace](https://ukgbc.org/resources/reused-materials-exchange-marketplace/))
- Structural steel, SCI demolition-contractor survey (repeated 2012): heavy structural sections and tubes about 7% reused and 93% recycled; across all steel construction products about 5% reused, 91% recycled and 4% lost. ([SteelConstruction.info, recycling and reuse survey](https://steelconstruction.info/topics/sustainability/the-recycling-and-reuse-survey/); [SteelConstruction.info, recycling and reuse](https://www.steelconstruction.info/Recycling_and_reuse))
- Structural steel, UKGBC (December 2022): 99% of UK structural steel sections are recovered, with 86% recycled (typically in electric arc furnaces) and 13% reused. **Conflict flag:** this is far above SCI's 5 to 7%. The UKGBC figure comes from an event wrap-up, not a methodological study. ([UKGBC, Circular Steel event wrap-up, Dec 2022](https://ukgbc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Circular-Steel-Event-Wrap-Up.pdf))
- Reuse is falling over the long run: ReLondon reports that material reuse tonnage declined by 25% between 1998 and 2007. ([ReLondon, Re-usable buildings: an exploration of the business case](https://relondon.gov.uk/resources/report-re-usable-buildings-an-exploration-of-the-business-case))

**London**
- Mace ("Closing the circle"): 90.2% of demolition and construction waste from the City of London and from London more broadly is recovered for some form of reuse or recycling, but most of it does not return to the construction sector. ([The Construction Index on the Mace report](https://www.theconstructionindex.co.uk/news/view/mace-calls-for-london-to-move-to-circular-construction); [Mace report](https://view.ceros.com/mace/circularity-report-page))
- London Plan waste forecasts (older London Aggregates Working Party paper): London CD&E arisings of 9.75 to 13 Mt a year by 2041. The same paper calls London CD&E data "hitherto unreliable". This is dated and is a forecast. ([GLA, London Aggregates Working Party paper](https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/London%20aggregate%20Working%20Party%20%281387%29.pdf))
- City of London exported about 347,414 t of waste in 2022, most of it CD&E (search extract of a borough duty-to-cooperate document). ([City of London, Duty to cooperate: waste, City Plan 2040](https://cityoflondon.gov.uk/assets/Services-Environment/duty-to-cooperate-waste-city-plan-2040.pdf))
- An earlier London Plan aggregates policy set a target of 95% recycling or reuse of CD&E waste by 2020. ([GLA, Policy 5.20 Aggregates](https://www.london.gov.uk/node/15202))
- ReLondon: the built environment accounts for over 10% of London's consumption-based emissions and is the city's biggest producer of waste. ([ReLondon, built environment](https://relondon.gov.uk/built-environment))

### Inferences
- The supply pool by tonnage is large (about 63 Mt a year in England), but more than 90% of it is low-value mineral fraction (concrete, masonry, soils) that is already "recovered" as aggregate. The reusable, higher-value fraction (steel sections, raised access floor, facade stone, glazing, timber, ceiling tiles, sanitaryware, mechanical and electrical plant) is a small share by mass. For sizing, the useful number is the reuse share (about 4% by UKGBC) applied to value, not to tonnage.
- Rough top-down cross-check: 4% of 63.3 Mt is about 2.5 Mt a year in England. At the one realised resale value found (about £248 per tonne, see question 3), that is about £0.6bn a year of goods that are, or could be, reused. The figure mixes definitions (UKGBC's 4% is of materials, not waste) and should be treated as an order of magnitude only.
- Because policy reporting (GLA CE statements) measures "reuse, recycling and recovery" together against a 95% target, current London rules can be met without any reuse. Real reuse demand comes from the carbon policies (WLC, Carbon Options, Retrofit First) and client targets rather than from waste targets.

### Gaps
- The Defra 2025/2026 edition tables (England 2022 final and 2024 provisional, with the C&D excluding excavation split) could not be opened. Verify 63.0 / 59.4 / 93.4% for 2022 and the 2024 provisional numbers.
- No UK-level 2022 or 2024 non-hazardous C&D figure was found. The last UK figure on the page is 2020.
- No current London-wide annual C&D tonnage (excluding excavation) was found. London Environment Strategy and GLA waste-needs evidence may hold one.
- No official statistic separates "reuse" from "recycling" in C&D waste. All reuse shares are survey or industry estimates.
- An often-quoted claim that demolition and construction cause 40 to 50 MtCO2e a year of embodied emissions (attributed to SAVE Britain's Heritage in search results) could not be tied to a primary source.

## 2. How many commercial buildings (especially offices) are demolished or stripped out each year, and how large is refurbishment and fit-out churn?

### Takeaway
There is no official count of UK or London commercial demolitions. The 50,000 buildings a year figure is an advocacy estimate. The best London proxy is the Deloitte London Office Crane Survey: in 2025, 4.8m sq ft (about 446,000 m2) of office construction started, of which 3.1m sq ft (about 288,000 m2) were refurbishments and 1.6m sq ft (about 149,000 m2) new build. Fit-out is a separate, larger churn: the UK interior fit-out and refurbishment market was about £9.85bn in 2025, a quarter of it offices.

### Cited Findings
**Demolition counts (UK)**
- "Over 50,000 buildings are demolished each year" in the UK. The figure is widely repeated by RIBA, the Architects' Journal RetroFirst campaign and others, with no primary government dataset behind it. Estimate. ([Real Asset Insight reporting RIBA, 2021](https://realassetinsight.com/2021/07/12/riba-demolitions-should-be-stopped-to-lower-emissions/); [Thirdway on AJ RetroFirst](https://thirdway.com/article/thirdway-signs-up-to-aj-retrofirst-campaign); [ICE Engineering Sustainability](https://www.emerald.com/jensu/article/172/3/119/428610))
- Context: one engineering firm says 80% of buildings that will exist in 2050 already stand; another source says almost 90%. ([EOC Engineers](https://www.eocengineers.com/?p=21532))

**Demolition industry size (conflicting)**
- IBISWorld gives three different figures for UK demolition in 2026: about £1.3bn (SIC 43.11, 815 businesses), £1.6bn on another page, and £27.1bn (58,985 businesses, a much broader scope that includes site preparation). These are commercial estimates and inconsistent with each other. ([IBISWorld, Demolition 2480](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-kingdom/industry/demolition/2480/); [IBISWorld, market size](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-kingdom/market-size/demolition/); [IBISWorld, 200552](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-kingdom/industry/demolition/200552/))
- Glenigan (2022): the pipeline of projects that include demolition work and gained planning permission in the previous two years was worth £61.5bn (a project value, not demolition turnover). ([Glenigan](https://www.glenigan.com/strong-prospects-for-demolition-for-construction-projects/))

**London office development and refurbishment (Deloitte London Office Crane Survey, central London)**
- 2025 (published April 2026): 4.8m sq ft of starts across 57 schemes, down from 7.5m sq ft in 2024 and 8.7m sq ft in 2023, against a five-year average of 6.5m sq ft. ([Deloitte press release, April 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/about/press-room/deloitte-london-office-crane-survey-april-2026.html); [Specfinish summary](https://specfinish.co.uk/deloitte-london-office-crane-survey/); [Building Design: new office starts fell 35% in 2025](https://www.bdonline.co.uk/news/new-office-construction-starts-fell-35-in-london-in-2025-deloitte-survey-shows/5141971.article))
- 2025: refurbishments were 3.1m sq ft of starts (66%), down from 3.8m sq ft in 2024. New-build starts fell to 1.6m sq ft from 3.6m sq ft in 2024. Refurbishments have outpaced new build throughout the post-Covid period. ([Specfinish](https://specfinish.co.uk/deloitte-london-office-crane-survey/))
- 2025: 7.1m sq ft of office space was delivered, up 8% on 2024. ([Deloitte, April 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/about/press-room/deloitte-london-office-crane-survey-april-2026.html))
- Summer 2024: a record 16.4m sq ft under construction across 127 schemes. ([Deloitte, "Refurbs powering London office"](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/about/press-room/london-crane-survey-refurbs-powering-london-office.html))
- First half of 2025: over 83% of about 2.4m sq ft of starts were refurbishments. This is a shorter period, so it differs from the full-year 66%. ([Elite Agent](https://eliteagent.com/refurb-boom-in-london-as-eco-demand-outpaces-supply/))

**London office stock, obsolescence and retrofit need**
- London office stock is about 300m sq ft (about 27.9m m2). New retrofit starts are only about 1% of it a year; the author argues about 7% a year is needed. 91% of offices across eight major UK cities are rated EPC C or worse. ([Estates Gazette](https://www.estatesgazette.co.uk/?p=1209537))
- Knight Frank (May 2024): 97.6m sq ft of London offices (about 38% of stock) are rated below EPC C, which would make them unlettable from 2027 without works, and 168.8m sq ft are below the proposed 2030 EPC B threshold. 10.6m sq ft (4% of stock) is potentially suitable for repurposing. ([Knight Frank, Insight 6: Retrofit or repurpose](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2024/5/insight-6-retrofit-or-repurpose))
- Knight Frank (March 2025): the most comprehensive retrofit costs £70 to £110 per sq ft (about £750 to £1,180 per m2), or £11.8bn to £18.9bn to bring the whole non-compliant London office market up to standard. Approvals for office refurbishments fell to a six-year low, from 500 in 2019 to under 200 in 2024, partly explained by falling approval rates (search extract). ([Knight Frank, Meeting the commercial property retrofit challenge](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2025/3/meeting-the-commercial-property-retrofit-challenge))
- **Conflict flag:** Savills says 14.8m sq ft of London offices are rated C or below, 70% of current supply, with 6.6m sq ft second-hand and not refurbished in over a decade. This measures available supply, not total stock, so it is not comparable with Knight Frank's figures. ([Savills blog](https://www.savills.co.uk/blog/article/343465/commercial-property/recognising-the-environmental-advantage-of-opting-for-older-offices.aspx))
- The MEES EPC C (2027) and EPC B (2030) dates were proposals, and it remains unclear what dates the current government will set. ([Howard Kennedy](https://www.howardkennedy.com/latest/article/retrofitting-buildings-for-a-sustainable-future))

**Fit-out churn (Cat A and Cat B)**
- Barbour ABI: UK interior refurbishment and fit-out market £9.85bn in 2025, up 4% on 2024 at current prices. Scope is Cat A and Cat B fit-out of non-residential buildings, excluding minor RMI. Split: commercial offices 25%, education 23%, leisure 20%, healthcare 12%, retail 11%, other public 9%. Commercial estimate. ([Barbour ABI](https://barbour-abi.com/public-sector-spend-sustainability-push-uk-fit-out-refurbishment-market-9-85bn/))
- Overbury (a leading fit-out contractor), FY2025: London was 79% of income (2024: 74%) and commercial office 86% of revenue. ([Construction Wave, March 2026](https://constructionwave.co.uk/2026/03/09/overbury-enjoys-significant-growth-in-london-fitout-sector/))
- Turner and Townsend: the average high-specification office fit-out in London costs US$5,872 per m2, the world's second most expensive. ([Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/44287/london-now-worlds-second-most-expensive-office-fit-out-market))
- Example scale: a £75m office fit-out at the M Building, Oxford Street, for BDO UK. ([Glenigan](https://www.glenigan.com/boom-in-fit-out-work-set-to-keep-contractors-busy-indoors/))
- UK office interiors produce about 77 kg of waste per m2 per fit-out cycle (RESET 2022, cited in a 2026 review). ([Piadeh et al., ECAM 2026](https://doi.org/10.1108/ECAM-05-2025-0742))
- Strip-out cost benchmarks (contractor marketing pages, indicative only): London office strip-out £8 to £25 per m2 (2026); soft strip £15 to £40 per m2 and full strip £50 to £120 per m2 or more. ([Strip Out Company](https://stripoutcompany.co.uk/marketing/how-much-does-a-strip-out-cost-in-london); [Ashbarn](https://ashbarnconstruction.co.uk/what-is-a-strip-out-in-construction-a-guide-for-commercial-property-owners/))

**Marks and Spencer, Oxford Street (the "retrofit first" test case)**
- July 2023: Michael Gove refused permission against the inspector's recommendation, citing heritage and embodied carbon. Demolition would have released almost 40,000 t of embodied carbon. ([JMW](https://www.jmw.co.uk/blog/commercial-litigation-dispute-resolution/embodied-carbon-and-the-ms-oxford-street-decision); [Hogan Lovells](https://hlc.com/en/publications/dividing-opinion-uk-secretary-of-state-refuses-ms-oxford-street-scheme-against-inspectors-advice))
- High Court: Mrs Justice Lieven upheld five of M&S's six grounds and quashed the refusal, finding the letter "thoroughly confused" on whether carbon offsetting applied to embodied carbon. ([Construction Briefing](https://www.constructionbriefing.com/news/judge-decision-to-halt-demolition-of-flagship-ms-store-was-unlawful/8035674.article))
- Angela Rayner, as Secretary of State, later granted permission to demolish and redevelop, finding no viable or deliverable alternative to retention and that embodied carbon harm would arise from either scheme. The exact date was not confirmed in search extracts; it is believed to be 2025. Commentators disagree on whether the case sets a precedent. ([Pinsent Masons, Out-Law](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/m-s-decision-nppf-update-embodied-carbon-issue); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=1953594))

### Inferences
- London office flows converted to m2 (author's arithmetic): 2025 starts about 446,000 m2 (refurbishment about 288,000 m2, new build about 149,000 m2); 2024 starts about 697,000 m2 (refurbishment about 353,000 m2, new build about 334,000 m2); 2023 about 808,000 m2; five-year average about 604,000 m2 a year.
- Donor supply proxy for central London: almost every refurbishment start involves a full Cat A and Cat B strip-out (raised floors, ceilings, partitions, mechanical and electrical plant, often facade). Most new-build starts in central London replace a demolished or "deep" stripped building. So roughly 450,000 to 800,000 m2 of central London office floor a year passes through strip-out or demolition at the base-build level, before counting tenant fit-out churn. This excludes outer London, retail, hotels and other commercial uses, so it understates total London supply.
- Office fit-out spend is about £2.5bn a year in the UK (25% of £9.85bn, author's arithmetic). Every new Cat B fit-out implies a strip-out of the previous one, which feeds raised access floor, ceiling tile, partition, glass and furniture supply.
- Knight Frank's refurbishment and retrofit backlog (97.6m to 168.8m sq ft below EPC C or B) points to a sustained pipeline of strip-outs to 2030 if MEES dates hold, but the dates are uncertain.

### Gaps
- No count of commercial or office demolitions per year for the UK or London, and no demolished floor area per year, was found. The GLA Planning London Datahub (completions with demolished floorspace by use class) is the likely primary source but could not be accessed.
- NFDC membership, turnover, demolition project counts and waste or reuse statistics were not found.
- The Deloitte under-construction total for 2025/2026 and the scheme counts for refurbishments versus new build were not found.
- A London-only fit-out market value and annual Cat A or Cat B floor area were not found. The Barbour ABI full report is paywalled.
- The date and conditions of the 2025 M&S permission need checking against the decision letter.

## 3. Recoverable material quantities and values per m2 of demolished commercial floor area; reclaimed versus new prices; published estimates of value lost

### Takeaway
Hard per-m2 data is thin. Steel-framed offices hold about 55 to 110 kg of primary structural steel per m2 of GIFA. One audit found about 0.85 t of all materials per m2. One realised London reuse sale gave about £248 per tonne. Reclaimed steel has sold at about £850 per tonne against about £2,200 per tonne for new fabricated steel, but reuse adds about £368 per tonne of extra cost (SCI, about 2016 prices). No published estimate of the £ value of materials lost in UK demolition each year was found.

### Cited Findings
**Quantities per m2**
- Primary structural steel in UK steel-framed offices: about 55 kg/m2 GIFA (low rise, short spans), 90 kg/m2 (high rise, long spans) and 110 kg/m2 (complex high rise). Secondary steelwork (risers, lifts, cladding framing) is excluded. ([SteelConstruction.info, cost planning multi-storey offices](https://steelconstruction.info/topics/cost-of-structural-steelwork/cost-planning-multi-storey-offices); [SCI Costing Steelwork](https://www.SteelConstruction.info/images/9/90/Costing_Steelwork-1.pdf))
- SCI cost benchmarks, Q4 2025 (BCIS index 100 basis): a 55 kg/m2 low-rise frame costs £159 to £191 per m2, and a 90 kg/m2 high-rise frame £266 to £301 per m2. ([SteelConstruction.info, cost planning](https://steelconstruction.info/topics/cost-of-structural-steelwork/cost-planning-multi-storey-offices))
- A pre-demolition audit of five London buildings totalling 33,000 m2 identified 28,100 t of material (about 0.85 t/m2) and set a target of 29% on-site reuse by volume. Search extract; the hosting page is one of the GLA pre-demolition audit pages below and was not confirmed. ([Hodkinson, GLA mandated audits](https://www.hodkinsonconsultancy.com/gla-mandated-audits-for-circular-economy-statements/); [Sustainable Stories, pre-demolition audits in London](https://www.sustainablestories.uk/articles/pre-demolition-audits-a-practical-step-towards-sustainable-construction-in-london))
- Fit-out waste is about 77 kg per m2 per fit-out cycle in UK offices. ([Piadeh et al., ECAM 2026, citing RESET 2022](https://doi.org/10.1108/ECAM-05-2025-0742))
- UK in-use stock: more than 5 billion tonnes of concrete and 500 Mt of steel in the built environment. Intensity coefficients can vary by up to a factor of 100 between archetypes. ([Liverpool/Exeter, Resources, Conservation and Recycling, doi 10.1016/j.resconrec.2021.105821](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3137701/))

**Realised value from a real strip-out**
- Material Index pre-demolition audit of two London buildings (including 1 Plough Place, three floors): 68.43 t identified for reuse, and sales coordinated across eight reuse partners brought in just over £17,000, about £248 per tonne (author's arithmetic). Floor area was not given. ([UKGBC, Reused Materials Exchange Marketplace](https://ukgbc.org/resources/reused-materials-exchange-marketplace/))

**Structural steel: reclaimed versus new**
- SCI historical analysis: the average price gap between new sections and scrap was £313 per tonne over 2000 to 2016. A separate SCI report gives a 15-year average gap of £280 per tonne, never below £200 per tonne and peaking at about £520 per tonne. ([SCI Report 1, Barriers to structural steel reuse (ASBP, 2016)](https://asbp.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Barriers-to-structural-steel-reuse-and-means-of-overcoming-them-SCI-Report-1.pdf); [PBC Today](https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/building-control-news/reusing-structural-steel/62082/))
- SCI extra costs of reuse, for a typical multi-storey commercial building at about 2016 prices: deconstruction £161 per tonne (75% of erection cost), extra fabrication £120 per tonne, transport and storage £34 per tonne, and testing £53 per tonne (20% of beams tested at £60 each). That totals about £368 per tonne (author's sum). SCI concluded mainstream reuse was not viable except in niche or project-specific cases. ([SCI Report 1](https://asbp.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Barriers-to-structural-steel-reuse-and-means-of-overcoming-them-SCI-Report-1.pdf))
- Case study (small domestic refurbishment, date not shown): 1.45 t of reclaimed steel bought at about £850 per tonne. With processing and fabrication it cost £1,300+ per tonne excluding VAT and delivery, against about £2,200 per tonne for new steel including fabrication and painting, a 41% saving. A single small project. ([ASBP case study](https://asbp.org.uk/case-studies/steel-reuse-domestic-refurbishment-project))
- Bath/Cambridge peer-reviewed study (2018): reused steel is generally somewhat more expensive than new, except where elements come from a nearby site or testing can be avoided. ([Univ. of Bath research portal](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/options-to-make-steel-reuse-profitable-an-analysis-of-cost-and-ri/); [Use Less Group](https://www.uselessgroup.org/outreach/publications/all-papers/options-make-steel-reuse-profitable-analysis-cost-and-risk))
- Carbon: reused UK steel is about 50 kgCO2e per tonne, about 97% less than blast-furnace steel (comment piece). ([PBC Today](https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/building-control-news/reusing-structural-steel/62082/))
- Project scale: one project sourcing nearly 450 t of reclaimed steel was described as the biggest UK steel reuse project at the time. Another London project reused about 60 to 70 t of a 100 to 120 t frame (search extract, January 2025 article). ([New Civil Engineer, "Why is steel reuse uptake low"](https://www.newcivilengineer.com/in-depth/why-is-steel-reuse-uptake-low-despite-proven-benefits-31-01-2025/))
- ASBP-linked steel reuse project estimate: a UK steel reuse market worth £12.5m a year if barriers were overcome (search extract; attribution and date not confirmed). ([PBC Today, The significance of steel re-use](https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/plant-equipment-supplies-news/significance-steel-re-use-2/20631/))

**Raised access floor**
- Tate (Kingspan) sells reclaimed steel-encapsulated, wood-core "ReUse" panels with up to a 25-year limited warranty when installed on new pedestals. It claims a 72% reduction in embodied carbon per m2 against its new RMG600+ (A to C, excluding pedestal). ([Tate ReUse panel](https://www.tateglobal.com/emea/products/raised-access-floors/reuse-panel/))
- RMF (UK) reuses panels on a new all-steel pedestal understructure, working with strip-out and demolition contractors across the UK. Panels are inspected, cleaned and randomly deflection-tested. No prices published. ([UKGBC, Raised Modular Flooring](https://ukgbc.org/resources/raised-modular-flooring/))
- The new comparator Kingspan RMG600+ has at least 86% recycled content and a 57% carbon reduction per panel against the earlier RMG600. ([RIBA Journal](https://ribaj.com/products/kingspan-lowest-ever-embodied-carbon-raised-access-floor-panel-rmg600-plus))

**Brick (facade and walling)**
- Reclaimed brick listings (supplier, 2024): £1.20 per brick (£60 per m2, £1,200 per 1,000), £1.22 per brick (£70.76 per m2) and £1.68 per brick (£84 per m2, £1,680 per 1,000). ([Brickhunter batch 27](https://brickhunter.com/reclaimed-bricks/reclaimed-batch-27-red-clay-brick); [batch 26](https://brickhunter.com/reclaimed-bricks/reclaimed-batch-26-red-clay-brick); [batch 28](https://brickhunter.com/reclaimed-bricks/reclaimed-batch-28-red-clay-brick))

([Gardiners Reclaims](https://gardinersreclaims.co.uk/?p=41594); [Gardiners brick cost](https://gardinersreclaims.co.uk/how-much-does-a-brick-cost-and-where-to-buy-a-brick-in-the-uk/); [Brickhunter](https://brickhunter.com/blog/how-much-do-bricks-cost-))

**Value of materials lost**
- No sourced UK figure for the £ value of materials lost in demolition each year was found. Holcim UK argues qualitatively that buildings are demolished while their materials still hold economic value and embodied carbon. ([Holcim UK](https://www.holcim.co.uk/news-and-resources/press-releases/Building-it-Twice-Podcast))
- The UK reclamation trade has not been valued recently. Salvo ran BRE-managed surveys in 1998 and 2007, and an Interreg FCRBE statistical report (2021 to 2022) covers the UK, Ireland, Benelux and France. The UK salvage sector is mostly small local firms, and reclaimed volumes have decreased. ### Inferences
- Worked unit economics for a 10,000 m2 GIA steel-framed office (author's arithmetic from the figures above; indicative only):
  - Primary steel: 550 to 1,100 t. At about £850 per tonne reclaimed, that is a gross material value of about £0.47m to £0.94m. The new fabricated equivalent at about £2,200 per tonne is £1.21m to £2.42m. The new-versus-scrap gap of about £280 to £313 per tonne is the room that reuse has to cover its extra costs of about £368 per tonne (2016 prices), which is why steel reuse is marginal without a carbon premium or a nearby receiving site.
  - Total material: about 8,500 t, at the 0.85 t/m2 audit ratio, mostly concrete and masonry.
  - Fit-out layer per cycle: about 770 t (77 kg/m2). At the Material Index realised average of about £248 per tonne, that is roughly £190,000, or about £19 per m2 of floor, if all were sold.
- Per m2 of demolished or stripped office, the realistic recoverable resale value seems to sit in the tens of pounds per m2 (about £19 per m2 for fit-out items; about £47 to £94 per m2 of GIFA for steel at reclaimed price if the whole frame were reused, which it rarely is). For an 8% commission, Tallyard's revenue per m2 of donor floor would be roughly £1.50 to £7.50, before storage, testing and transport margins.
- Applied to the London office proxy in question 2 (about 149,000 to 334,000 m2 a year of new-build starts, taken as demolished frames), steel content would be about 8,200 to 36,800 t a year. At £850 per tonne, the gross value is about £7m to £31m a year if every frame were steel and fully reused. At a 7 to 13% reuse share, it is about £0.5m to £4m a year. Many 1960s to 1980s London offices are concrete-framed, so this overstates steel supply.
- Reclaimed products such as bricks and some stone sell at or above new prices. The buyer's case for them is carbon, heritage matching and planning, not cost. Tallyard's value proposition differs by material.

### Gaps
- No 2024 to 2026 price list for reclaimed structural steel from UK reuse stockholders. The searches turned up only 2016 to 2019 benchmarks and a single case study. A current new fabricated steel price per tonne (BCSA, 2025) was not retrieved because the search budget ran out.
- No price per m2 for reused against new raised access floor. Quotes from RMF and Tate on a like-for-like basis would be needed.
- No per-m2 quantities for raised access floor (typically about the net internal area), ceiling tiles, glazing or facade stone in demolished offices. No reclaimed York stone or Portland stone price per m2.
- No study gives recoverable resale value per m2 of demolished commercial floor. The Material Index case lacks floor area.
- The SCI P427 reuse protocol (2019) and more recent SCI steel reuse market reports were not reviewed in this session.

## 5. Policy drivers and their reach

### Takeaway
The binding drivers in London are London Plan Policy SI 7 (Circular Economy Statements with pre-redevelopment audits) and Policy SI 2 (whole life-cycle carbon) for referable schemes, about 90 cases a year; the City of London Carbon Options Guidance for all major schemes; and Westminster's Retrofit First policy. Nationally there is still no statutory embodied carbon or reuse requirement: Part Z is an industry proposal, the UK Net Zero Carbon Buildings Standard (voluntary) launched v1 in March 2026, and the Circular Economy Growth Plan for England was still unpublished in September 2026. A draft new London Plan (consulted on July to October 2026) reportedly adds numeric upfront carbon targets.

### Cited Findings
**London Plan SI 7 and the Circular Economy Statement**
- All referable applications (outline, detailed or hybrid) must submit a Circular Economy Statement covering reuse of demolition materials, design to reduce material demand, waste management and monitoring. The London Plan Guidance was published 25 March 2022. ([GLA, Circular Economy Statement guidance](https://www.london.gov.uk/programmes-strategies/planning/implementing-london-plan/london-plan-guidance/circular-economy-statement-guidance); [GLA LPG PDF](https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/circular_economy_statements_lpg_0.pdf))
- Targets include 95% reuse, recycling or recovery of C&D waste. Applicants are encouraged to commit to percentages of reused materials on site and to report waste streams in tonnes. ([GLA LPG PDF](https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/circular_economy_statements_lpg_0.pdf))
- Reach: in calendar 2023 there were 93 referable cases determined, and the GLA received 71 Circular Economy Statements for them (CE Monitoring Report 2023, published November 2024). ([GLA, Circular Economy Monitoring 2023](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/107727/download))
- The 2022 monitoring report covers CE statements submitted by the end of 2022 and has a dedicated "Pre-Demolition Audits" section. It was published May/June 2024. Its figures could not be extracted. ([GLA, Circular Economy Monitoring 2022](https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-06/Circular-Economy-Monitoring-2022-FINAL-June-2024.pdf))
- Pre-redevelopment and pre-demolition audits are mandated as part of GLA CE statements. ([Hodkinson Consultancy](https://www.hodkinsonconsultancy.com/gla-mandated-audits-for-circular-economy-statements/)); UKGBC publishes a pre-demolition audit tool ([UKGBC](https://ukgbc.org/resources/pre-demolition-audit-tool/))
- The referable thresholds include more than 150 residential units (Mayor of London Order 2008); other thresholds were not verified in this session. ([DHA Planning](https://www.dhaplanning.co.uk/knowledge/looking-beyond-the-mands-decision))

**Whole life-cycle carbon (London Plan SI 2 F)**
- WLC assessments apply to applications referred to the Mayor; non-referable majors are encouraged. The WLC guidance was adopted March 2022. The City of London's own monitoring of applications approved January 2021 to March 2023 estimated a 60-year WLC impact of about 1,576 ktCO2e. ([City of London, WLC monitoring committee report, July 2023](https://democracy.cityoflondon.gov.uk/documents/s188808/WLC%20carbon%20monitoring%20data%20-%20Committee%20Report%2018%20July%202023.pdf))

**City of London Carbon Options Guidance (COG) Planning Advice Note**
- Adopted by the Planning and Transportation Committee in March 2023 as an interim measure. Carbon optioneering is required for all major schemes, and for minor schemes not retaining at least 50% of the existing building. The 50% test changed from mass (2023) to floor area (update). ([Buildington](https://www.buildington.co.uk/blog/should-you-retrofit-or-demolish-a-london-building); [City of London COG review report](https://democracy.cityoflondon.gov.uk/documents/s226466/04%20COGR%20report.pdf))
- Revision 02 (December 2025, per Buildington) requires a Carbon Options Tool and Carbon Options Study at pre-application, covering retention and demolition rates, upfront and whole life carbon. A scheme is exempt if it retains all substructure and reuses at least 80% of superstructure floor area. ([Buildington](https://www.buildington.co.uk/blog/should-you-retrofit-or-demolish-a-london-building))
- Retrofit first is moving into the draft City Plan 2040. ([Montagu Evans](https://www.montagu-evans.co.uk/articles/retrofit-first-first-glimpse-of-the-city-of-londons-new-draft-city-plan-2040/); [Lichfields, "Retrofit first: the City, Camden, now Westminster", April 2024](https://lichfields.uk/blog/2024/april/02/retrofit-first-the-city-camden-now-westminster-who-will-be-next))

**Westminster Retrofit First (City Plan partial review, Policy 43)**
- Demolition is acceptable only where the applicant shows, in sequence, that the building is unsafe, that the required use cannot be supported, that WLC analysis shows lower impact, or that public benefits outweigh the extra carbon. A third-party-reviewed pre-redevelopment audit is required. Adoption was expected by the end of 2025, having slipped from early 2025. ([Elliott Wood](https://www.elliottwood.co.uk/latest/how-westminster-reframed-the-retrofit-conversation); [WPA retrofit workshop slides](https://www.westminsterpropertyassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/Retrofit-workship-slidedeck-2.pdf); [Estates Gazette, 2 August 2025](https://eg.mydigitalpublication.co.uk/articles/ian-poole))
- To count as a retrofit, 50% of existing floor area must be retained (one source). Carbon offset payments would be up to nine times current levels. ([IHBC](https://newsblogs.ihbc.org.uk/?p=39737); [Building Design](https://www.bdonline.co.uk/news/westminster-council-unveils-plans-to-become-a-retrofit-first-city/5128145.article))
- In October 2025 the Westminster Property Association called the draft Retrofit First Policy Guidance "the most challenging aspect of delivering long-term and sustainable development". ([WPA response, October 2025](https://www.westminsterpropertyassociation.com/wp-content/uploads/WPA-Response-to-WCC-Retrofit-First-Guidance-FINAL.pdf))

**New London Plan (draft)**
- The draft was published 16 July 2026, with consultation to 15 October 2026. It reportedly introduces numeric upfront embodied carbon targets per m2 for the first time, and "retrofit first" is "about to exist in the London Plan". Single blog source; check against the GLA draft. ([Buildington](https://www.buildington.co.uk/blog/should-you-retrofit-or-demolish-a-london-building))

**National**
- UK Net Zero Carbon Buildings Standard: the pilot version was published September 2024 and Version 1 launched 20 March 2026 (voluntary). In the pilot, 67% of 106 survey respondents said the upfront embodied carbon limits for new works were achievable. V1 added shell-and-core upfront limits for industrial and logistics buildings. ([Cundall](https://www.cundall.com/news/the-uk-net-zero-carbon-building-standard-is-finally-here); [Ridge, 2026](https://ridge.co.uk/news/uk-net-zero-carbon-buildings-standard-what-developers-need-to-know-in-2026/); [BCIS](https://www.bcis.co.uk/?p=14128); [CIBSE Journal](https://www.cibsejournal.com/general/piloting-the-uk-net-zero-carbon-buildings-standard/))
- Part Z is an industry proposal, not government policy. It would require WLC assessment for buildings over about 1,000 m2 or 10 dwellings, with reporting and then limits; one source says reporting by 2026 and limits by 2028. A private member's bill inspired by Part Z was tabled, with progress unknown. Backers include ICE, CIBSE, CIOB and IStructE. ([Designing Buildings](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/The_Net_Zero_Carbon_Buildings_Standard_and_Part_Z_explained); [Thornton Tomasetti](https://www.thorntontomasetti.com/news/uk-building-regulations-part-z-whole-life-carbon-and-embodied-carbon-reporting); [One Click LCA](https://oneclicklca.com/en/resources/articles/part-z-whole-life-carbon-rules-for-uk-construction); [IStructE](https://www.istructe.org/resources/news/embodied-carbon-regulation/))
- Circular Economy Growth Plan for England: first expected autumn 2025, then "early 2026", then "spring 2026", and still unpublished as of September 2026. A parliamentary answer on 10 September 2026 said the government "intends to publish". Construction is a priority sector, with sources giving five or six sectors in total. In May 2026 SUEZ led an open letter from 50 organisations calling for publication. ([Karmactive, September 2026](https://www.karmactive.com/uk-circular-economy-growth-plan-delay/); [Letsrecycle](https://www.letsrecycle.com/news/circular-economy-strategy-now-expected-in-2026/); [EIC, 2025](https://eic-uk.co.uk/media/abnbt5qn/eic-circular-economy_2025.pdf); [Brighton and Hove pathfinder](https://mta-sts.brighton-hove.gov.uk/news/2026/city-shows-way-circular-economy))
- MEES: EPC C by 2027 and EPC B by 2030 for commercial lettings were proposals, and the final dates remain uncertain. ([Howard Kennedy](https://www.howardkennedy.com/latest/article/retrofitting-buildings-for-a-sustainable-future); [Knight Frank 2024](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2024/5/insight-6-retrofit-or-repurpose))

### Inferences
- Policy reach today is concentrated. About 70 to 95 referable London schemes a year file CE statements with pre-redevelopment audits (93 cases and 71 statements in 2023). Every major City of London scheme runs carbon optioneering. Westminster requires third-party-reviewed pre-redevelopment audits. These schemes are the "must engage" donor and receiver pool for a London-first launch, and they produce exactly the audit data (material inventories in tonnes) that Tallyard ingests.
- The audits mandate inventory and reporting, not reuse. The 95% target is met by recycling. Reuse demand therefore depends on carbon limits: the draft new London Plan's numeric upfront carbon targets (if adopted, likely 2027 or later), NZCBS uptake by clients, and the City and Westminster retrofit-first tests. The draft London Plan is the single most important near-term step change for the 2030 serviceable market.
- No national mandate before about 2028 at the earliest. Part Z is not adopted, the CE Growth Plan is unpublished, and NZCBS is voluntary. Outside London, SAM by 2030 rests on voluntary corporate targets and borough-level policies.

### Gaps
- The full referable thresholds (Mayor of London Order 2008) were not verified. From the author's background knowledge, they include over 150 units, over 30 m height outside the City, and non-residential floorspace above 15,000 m2 outside central London; check before use.
- Aggregate pre-demolition audit outcomes (tonnes audited, % reuse committed) from the GLA 2022 and 2023 monitoring reports could not be extracted.
- The number of GLA WLC assessments a year and the GLA WLC monitoring report were not found.
- The government's position on an embodied carbon consultation (MHCLG, 2025 to 2026) and the NPPF wording on embodied carbon after December 2024 were not researched because the search budget ran out.
- Westminster's adoption date for Policy 43 was not confirmed.
- Retention versus demolition shares of approved City of London schemes since the COG was introduced were not found.

## 6. Industry estimates of reuse potential

### Takeaway
Quantified UK reuse-potential estimates are few and mostly London-focused. Mace estimates £1.25bn of materials (13.8 Mt) could be added to London's supply chain over a decade through circularity. Green Alliance calls for at least a one-third cut in construction raw material use by 2035. Reuse today is about 4% of materials (UKGBC) and 5 to 13% of steel sections. No national £ estimate of reuse potential was found.

### Cited Findings
- Mace, "Closing the circle": circular construction could add 13.8 Mt of building materials worth £1.25bn to London's construction supply chain over the next decade, saving 11 MtCO2. ([Development Finance Today](https://developmentfinancetoday.co.uk/article-desc-9761_London%20is%20the%20ideal%20place%20to%20build%20the%20world%E2%80%99s%20first%20circular%20construction%20economy,%20reports%20Mace); [Mace report](https://view.ceros.com/mace/circularity-report-page))
- Green Alliance (circular construction report): calls for a target to cut raw material use in construction by at least one third by 2035, and argues that cutting embodied carbon through material efficiency and reuse is increasingly a cost saving. ([Architectural Technology, CIAT](https://architecturaltechnology.com/resource/green-alliance-report-i-circular-construction-i-published.html); [edie](https://www.edie.net/report-uks-construction-sector-wont-reach-net-zero-without-circular-economy-focus/?amp=true)); a January 2026 Green Alliance paper covers circular economy growth and investment, but its figures were not extracted ([Green Alliance, January 2026](https://green-alliance.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/How-the-circular-economy-creates-growth-and-investment.pdf))
- LWARB (now ReLondon) estimated that circular economy principles could add "£3 billion" or more in growth for London by 2036. The figure is garbled in the citing document, so verify it before use. ([Circular Economy Statement citing LWARB, data.london.gov.uk](https://data.london.gov.uk/download/kensintgon-forum-hotel-application-documents-july-2020/57ff0b4b-6719-49fd-bfd0-90a5cc6d76eb/Circular%2520Economy%2520Statement.pdf))
- ReLondon CIRCuIT project aims: cut yearly virgin raw material consumption in new construction by 20% and show cost savings of 15%. ([ReLondon CIRCuIT case study](https://relondon.gov.uk/resources/case-study-circular-construction-in-regenerative-cities-circuit-project))
- UKGBC: about 2 to 6% of materials directly reused, average about 4%. ([UKGBC](https://ukgbc.org/resources/reused-materials-exchange-marketplace/)); 13% of structural steel sections reused (December 2022) ([UKGBC](https://ukgbc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Circular-Steel-Event-Wrap-Up.pdf)); SCI (2012) 5 to 7% ([SteelConstruction.info](https://steelconstruction.info/topics/sustainability/the-recycling-and-reuse-survey/))
- SCI (about 2016): mainstream structural steel reuse is not viable except in niche or project-specific cases, because the new-versus-scrap gap (average £280 to £313 per tonne) is smaller than the extra reuse costs. ([SCI Report 1](https://asbp.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Barriers-to-structural-steel-reuse-and-means-of-overcoming-them-SCI-Report-1.pdf))
- Barriers named by experts (NCE, January 2025): design and engineering complexity and the difficulty of establishing a supply chain. ([New Civil Engineer](https://www.newcivilengineer.com/in-depth/why-is-steel-reuse-uptake-low-despite-proven-benefits-31-01-2025/))
- UKGBC runs a Reused Materials Exchange Marketplace resource that documents brokered reuse (Material Index case: 68.43 t, just over £17,000 of sales across eight partners). ([UKGBC](https://ukgbc.org/resources/reused-materials-exchange-marketplace/))

### Inferences
- Mace's £1.25bn over ten years is about £125m a year of extra material supply in London (author's arithmetic). Taken as London's circular-materials GMV ceiling, an 8% take on it is about £10m a year. This is a useful top-down cross-check against the bottom-up London figures in questions 2 and 3, which reach tens of millions of pounds of GMV a year for steel alone if fully reused.
- The economics show that platform value lies in cutting the costs that sink reuse (testing, storage, transport, matching timing between donor and receiver), which SCI puts at about £368 per tonne for steel, more than the price gap. Tallyard's storage brokerage, testing referral and transport margin target exactly these costs, so SOM should be modelled per material, with steel needing a carbon premium or policy pull to clear.
- The near-term SAM (2026 to 2027) is London referable, City and Westminster schemes plus corporate-target clients. By 2030 the draft London Plan carbon targets, any national Part Z or NZCBS uptake and MEES-driven office strip-outs could widen it. The rise of reuse from about 4% towards the 10 to 20% implied by Green Alliance's and ReLondon's targets would be the main upside lever.

### Gaps
- No UK-wide £ estimate of reuse potential (UKGBC, WRAP, Arup, LETI or IStructE) with numbers was found. WRAP's construction work has not been updated in recent years, and LETI and IStructE publications reviewed in search results gave carbon targets rather than material or £ volumes.
- The ASBP estimate of a £12.5m-a-year steel reuse market lacks a confirmed date and method.
- The ReLondon business case for reusable buildings and any 2024 to 2026 ReLondon market sizing could not be read in full.
- Arup, IStructE and LETI reuse-potential numbers were not retrieved because the search budget ran out.
